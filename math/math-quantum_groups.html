Summary

Consider two primitive elements x, y. We try to impose a quantum deformation xy = qyx. But the coproduct is no longer multiplicative

Δ(x)Δ(y) = xy ⊗ 1 + x ⊗ y + y ⊗ x + 1 ⊗ xy = qyx ⊗ 1 + x ⊗ y + y ⊗ x + q(1⊗yx) Δ(x)Δ(y) − qΔ(y)Δ(x) = x ⊗ y + y ⊗ x − qx ⊗ y − qy ⊗ x

Dual Pairing

A dual pairing of bialgebras is a non-degenerate bilinear form such that ⟨xy, z⟩ = ∑⟨x, z(1)⟩⟨y, z(2)⟩ ⟨x, yz⟩ = ∑⟨x(1), y⟩⟨x(2), z⟩

and possibly ⟨S(x), y⟩ = ⟨x, S(y)⟩ ⟨1, y⟩ = ε(y) ⟨x, 1⟩ = ε(x)

A dual pairing between a Hopf algebra and itself is called a Laplace pairing, but I'll refer to any dual pairing as a Laplace pairing.

Coquasitriangular Structure

A Laplace pairing is called a coquasitriangular structure if it also satisfies ∑⟨x(1), y(1)⟩x(2)y(2) = ∑x(1)y(1)⟨x(2), y(2)⟩

Coregular Actions

Consider a dual pair of bialgebras ⟨A, B⟩ → k.

We have 4 different actions:

  1. Left action of A on B

⟨a, a′ ⇀ b⟩ = ⟨aa′, b⟩ = ∑⟨a, b(1)⟩⟨a′, b(2)⟩

a′ ⇀ b = ∑b(1)⟨a′, b(2)⟩

Mnemonic: a' passes through one factor of the coproduct and then pairs with the second factor.

  1. Right action of A on B

⟨a, b ↼ a′⟩ = ⟨a′a, b⟩ = ∑⟨a′, b(1)⟩⟨a, b(2)⟩

b ↼ a′ = ∑⟨a′, b(1)⟩b(2)

Mnemonic: a' passes through one factor of the coproduct and then pairs with the first factor.

  1. Left action of B on A

⟨b ⊳ a, b′⟩ = ⟨a, b′b⟩ = ∑⟨a(1), b′⟩⟨a(2), b⟩

since this holds for all b' we have b ⊳ a = ∑a(1)⟨a(2), b⟩

Mnemonic: b passes through one factor of the coproduct and then pairs with the second factor.

  1. Right action B on A

⟨a ⊲ b, b′⟩ = ⟨a, bb′⟩ = ∑⟨a(1), b⟩⟨a(2), b′⟩

a ⊲ b = ∑⟨a(1), b⟩a(2)

Mnemonic: b passes through one factor of the coproduct and then pairs with the first factor.

Order

Let's check that the ordering is appropriate to give us left/right actions

  1. ⟨a, a′ ⇀ (a″⇀b)⟩ = ⟨aa′, a″ ⇀ b⟩ = ⟨aa′a″, b⟩

  2. ⟨a, (b↼a′) ↼ a″⟩ = ⟨a″a, b ↼ a′⟩ = ⟨(a′a″)a, b⟩

  3. ⟨b′ ⊳ (b⊳a), b″⟩ = ⟨b ⊳ a, b″b′⟩ = ⟨a, b″(b′b)⟩

  4. ⟨(a⊲b) ⊲ b′, b″⟩ = ⟨a ⊲ b, b′b″⟩ = ⟨a, bb′b″⟩

So it's actually pretty easy to tell when you have to swap the order of things in the pairing; it happens when you have to move a and b past each other.

Intuition

Let H be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra and consider its algebraic dual H*. We have the evaluation pairing ⟨ϕ, g⟩ = ϕ(g)

The left and right coregular actions of H on H* are defined by ⟨g ⋅ ϕ, h⟩ = ⟨ϕ, hg⟩ and ⟨ϕ ⋅ g, h⟩ = ⟨ϕ, gh⟩. In other words, (g⋅ϕ)(h) = ϕ(hg) and (ϕ⋅g)(h) = ϕ(gh).

Since the pairing is non-degenerate, g ⋅ ϕ and ϕ ⋅ g are determined completely by how they pair with all of the h ∈ H.

Heisenberg Double

Note: Part of the confusion with these doubles is that you can define them with either orientation: if you have a pairing ⟨A, B⟩, you can get a double A ⊗ B and one B ⊗ A.

The Heisenberg doubles of a dual pair ⟨A, B⟩ are algebra structures on A ⊗ B and B ⊗ A given by

On A ⊗ B: [this is like a position representation] [Standard left Heisenberg double] (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑(b(1)⊳a) ⊗ b(2) = ∑a(1) ⊗ b(2)⟨a(2), b(1)⟩ [Standard right Heisenberg double] (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑(a⊲b(1)) ⊗ b(2) = ∑⟨a(1), b(1)⟩a(2) ⊗ b(2) [Standard right contraction] (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑a(1) ⊗ (b↼a(2)) = ∑⟨a(2), b(1)⟩a(1) ⊗ b(2) [Standard left contraction] (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑a(1) ⊗ (a(2)⇀b) = ∑⟨a(2), b(2)⟩a(1) ⊗ b(1)

Note: The 1st and 3rd options are the same (but i don't think they are in the graded case?)

and swapping (1) and (2): (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑(b(2)⊳a) ⊗ b(1) (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑(a⊲b(2)) ⊗ b(1) (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑a(2) ⊗ (b↼a(1)) (1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑a(2) ⊗ (a(1)⇀b)

On B ⊗ A: [this is like a momentum representation] (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑b(1) ⊗ (b(2)⊳a) (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑b(1) ⊗ (a⊲b(2)) (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑(a(1)⇀b) ⊗ a(2) (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑(b↼a(1)) ⊗ a(2)

and the twists (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑b(2) ⊗ (b(1)⊳a) (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑b(2) ⊗ (a⊲b(1)) (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑(a(2)⇀b) ⊗ a(1) (1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑(b↼a(2)) ⊗ a(1)

Preference

I'm going to stick with the right Heisenberg dual: it keeps A and B in the right order, and the numbers match up nicely

(1⊗b)(a⊗1) = ∑(a⊲b(1)) ⊗ b(2) = ∑⟨a(1), b(1)⟩a(2) ⊗ b(2)

Intuition

H* ⊗ H is the quantum analogue of the cotangent bundle or the Weyl algebra. It doesn't naturally have a Hopf algebra structure, but we can give it one.

We want (ϕ⊗1)(1⊗g) = ϕ ⊗ g and (1⊗g)(ϕ⊗1) = ∑⟨ϕ(2)g(1)⟩ϕ(1) ⊗ g(2)

Drinfel'd Double

(1⊗a)(b⊗1) = ∑⟨b(), S(a())⟩⟨b

Intuition

Unlike the Heisenberg double, the Drinfel'd double is designed to be a quasitriangular Hopf algebra

(1⊗g)(ϕ⊗1) = ∑⟨ϕ(1)S(g(1))⟩⟨ϕ(3)g(3)⟩ϕ(2) ⊗ g(2)
