You can make any finitely presented group

Gompf's Theorem

Every finitely presented group is the fundamental group of a compact, symplectic 4-manifold

Higher Dimensions

The result carries into higher dimensions: you can find a manifold of any dimension  ≥ 4 with a given fundamental group.

You basically look at M4 × Dk where Dk is a k-dimensional disk. The fundamental group is a monoidal functor over the product, so this preserves the fundamental group.

But this is a manifold with boundary. To remove the boundary, you attach handles.

Surgery Theory

In dimension  ≥ 5 you can show that manifolds with a fixed fundamental group G are classifyable. So for example, you can classify simply-connected manifolds in dimension  ≥ 5.

Markov (1958)

On Insolubility of the Homeomorphy Problem

This is one of my favourite mathematical results of all time.

Classifying compact manifolds up to homeomorphism is undecidable in dimension  ≥ 4.

First, Markov gives an algorithm that takes any group presentation and gives you a corresponding manifold. Your group is trivial if and only if the manifold you get is a homotopy 4-sphere (it has the same fundamental group and homology groups as S4 ). If your space is homeomorphic to S4 then you have a homotopy 4-sphere, and thus G is trivial. But the rest of the proof actually relies on the other direction: if you start with the trivial group, is the space you get from the algorithm actually homeomorphic to S4, or just a homotopy 4-sphere?

Markov then assumes the topological Poincare conjecture holds in dimension 4 (which wasn't proved at the time): that every homotopy 4-sphere is homeomorphic to $S4.$

  • it was proven in 1982 by Michael Freedman

Assuming that's true (which it turned out to be), if there was some invariant that could distinguish every manifold from S4, that would solve the word problem as follows:

  • take a presentation of your group
  • compute the manifold
  • you get S4 if and only if your group is trivial

Smooth Case

If you do everything smoothly, the trivial group gives you a smooth manifold that is homeomorphic to S4. But the Smooth 4D Poincare Conjecture (whether this implies that manifold is diffeomorphic to S4) is still unsolved.

  • if this conjecture isn't true, there are exotic smooth spheres

Homology is Decidable on CW Complexes

Homology is decidable both on the set of compact manifolds, and on finite CW complexes. Homotopy is undecidable on both of these.
