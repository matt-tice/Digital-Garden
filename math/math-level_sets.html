Summary

Level sets give a nice implicit way of defining shapes. Instead of having to work with complicated maps f : ℝm → ℝn, we reexpress them in the form g : ℝn → ℝ where g−1(c) = Im(f), for some chosen c (usually picked to be zero).

Except we can't usually do this. The codomain usually can't be made to just be scalars; in general you need it to be ℝn − m. But you also usually can't get the full domain — you can only do this locally (because you want g to be a smooth function, you can't just arbitrarily define it, so sometimes there's no way to make it capture all of the image of f).

  • also, the preimage of a point is going to be closed, since the point is closed, so if the image of f isn't closed, we can't capture it globally anyway

But notice something else: in doing this we are not capturing all the data of f

  • we're throwing away the domain data, in order to capture the image.
  • physically, we just care about the shape that f draws, not how it draws it.

  2. g−1(p) = Im(f) ∩ U
