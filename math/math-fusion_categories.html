Definitions

Dualizable object in a monoidal category

An object in a monoidal category A ∈ 𝒞 is right dualizable if there is an object A* and morphisms εA : A* ⊗ A → 1  ηA : 1 → A ⊗ A* such that ηA ⊗ 1A* ∘ α ∘ 1A* ⊗ εA = λA*−1 ∘ ρA* and 1A ⊗ ηA ∘ α ∘ εA ⊗ 1A = ρA−1 ∘ λA

Invertible: In the case that *ε, η* are isomorphisms, we call A an invertible object.

Terminology

Other people will call this a left dual. I'm using right dual to signify that the unit-like map η : 1 → A ⊗ A* puts the dual on the right of the tensor product, with the intuition that a unit should be something like the existence of the object.

Rigid monoidal category

A category 𝒞 is right rigid if every object has a right dual. Similarly it is left rigid if every object has a left dual. A rigid category is one that is both left and right rigid.

Simple object in a category with terminal objects

Let 𝒞 be a category with a terminal object •. An object A is simple if every coequalizer of the form E ⇉ A → Q has Q ≅ 1 or Q ≅ A

Semisimple cat

An abelian category 𝒞 is semisimple, if every object is a direct sum of finitely many simple objects.

Fusion category

A fusion category C is a rigid, semisimple, Vect-enriched monoidal category, with only finitely many simple objects (up to isomorphism), and such that the endomorphisms of the unit object just form the ground field k.

Intuition

The goal of a fusion category is to study how objects combine

Since every object can be written as a direct sum of finitely many simple objects, and there are only finitely many simple objects, Xi, we have a kind of structure coefficient equivalence that we can write:

Xi ⊗ Xj ≅ ⨁kNijkXk. where Nijk ∈ ℕ ≥ 0.

Ribbon category

A ribbon category is a rigid braided monoidal category (ℬ, ⊗, σ) along with a natural isomorphism θ : I → I from the identity functor to itself, called a twist such that

θA ⊗ B = σBA ∘ σAB ∘ θA ⊗ θB θ1 = id, where 1 is the monoidal unit θA* = θA*

Intuition

These categories look at how things knot and twist together

Twist: The twist operation is meant to account for the spin of a particle. As it turns out, the world lines of anyons shouldn't really be thought of as world lines, but rather world ribbons, where the extra dimension corresponds to the spin

Quantum groups: Finite-dimensional representations of a quantum group form ribbon categories.

Modular tensor category

A modular tensor category is a ribbon and fusion category 𝒞 which satisfies a non-degeneracy condition

Intuition

These are similar to ribbon categories, but prevent certain kinds of singularities that can show up in the knots

Let {Xi} be a set of representatives of the simple objects.

Trace: Let f : A → A be an endomorphism. We define the trace of f by

Tr(f) = ε ∘ (idA* ⊗ (f ∘ θA)) ∘ (idA ⊗ θA*) ∘ σA*A ∘ η

It's similar to the ordinary trace, but we have a twisting action on both strands. Note that Tr(f) : 1 → 1 is a member of the endomorphism ring of the vacuum, which by the definition of a fusion category, is isomorphic to the ground field. So Tr(f) ∈ k.

In the case that your category is the standard category of vector spaces, you get back the ordinary trace (because the twist is trivial there).

S-matrix: To form the Smatrix, we take the trace of a double braiding:

Sij = Tr(σAjAi ∘ σAiAj)

Non-degeneracy: The non-degeneracy condition is equivalent to the Smatrix being non-degenerate.

TQFTs: Modular tensor categories describe 2 + 1 dimensional topological quantum field theory.
