Chern-Simons Theory

Intro

This is the canonical example of a topological quantum field theory from a mathematical physics perspective.

It's based on Yang-Mills theory.

In Yang-Mills theory, we start with a smooth orientable, compact manifold M without boundary and a metric g, which we use to construct the Hodge star operator ⋆. The action takes the form

$S_{\text{Yang-Mills}} = \dfrac{1}{2g^{2}} \int_{M} Tr(F \wedge \star F)$

where F is the curvature 2-form F = dA + A ∧ A. In local coordinates this is Fμν = ∂μAν − ∂νAμ + [Aμ,Aν]

For Chern-Simons theory, we take a smooth, compact, oriented 3-manifold M without boundary, equipped with a principal G-bundle P, for a compact Lie group G.

We then take a connection 1-form A ∈ Ω1(M,𝔤). The Chern-Simons action is then

$S_{\text{Chern-Simons}} = \dfrac{k}{4 \pi } \int_{M} Tr(A \wedge dA + \dfrac{2}{3} A \wedge A \wedge A)$

Note: A is a Lie-algebra-valued differential form, not a scalar valued one. This is why we don't have A ∧ A = 0.

Lie-algebra forms: These have the general shape A = ∑iAidxi, where Ai ∈ 𝔤 are matrices.

Example

If A = A1dx1 + A2dx2, then A ∧ A = A1A2dx1 ∧ dx2 + A1A2dx2 ∧ dx1 + 0 + 0 = [A1,A2]dx1 ∧ dx2

Applying the Trace

$Tr(A \wedge B) = (-1)^{\abs{A} \abs{B}} Tr(B \wedge A)$.

In particular Tr(A∧4) = (−1)1 × 3Tr(A∧4), and so it must be zero.

Topological quantum field theory

We take closed, oriented 2-manifolds, and assign them finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, called the spaces of conformal blocks

Then the 3-manifolds (the cobordisms) give us a few things:

  • when we have the shape ∂iM = ∅, ∂fM = Σ, then our field theory is assigning a state vector of Hilbert space on Σ.
  • when ∂iM = ∅, ∂fM = ∅, we get a map ℂ → ℂ, which just picks out a single complex number. The is the partition function or topological invariant of the theory.

Chern-Simons

Consider a theory with M = Σ × ℝ. We write

Phase space: Ends up being the moduli space of flat connections on Σ modulo gauge transformations, which is finite-dimensional.

Geometric Quantization: We want to quantize this classical phase space. We pick a complex structure on Σ, turning it into a Riemann surface. The moduli space becomes a Kahler manifold. The Hilbert space ends up being a 0-cohomology (sheaf cohomology) of moduli space.

Kodaira Vanishing Theorem: Tells us that in this case the higher cohomology is zero.

    In the case that M has no boundary, we get that Z(M) assigns a number; this is the path integral

    Z(M) = ∫eiS[A]dA

    $Z(M) = \int \frac{\mathcal{D}A}{\text{Vol}(\text{Gauge})} \exp\left( i \frac{k}{4\pi} \int_M \text{Tr}\left(A \wedge dA + \frac{2}{3}A^3\right) \right)$

    When M = S3, we get the topological invariant of the sphere.

    This is the vacuum expectation value. But we can also look at higher correlation functions. This is where the Wilson loops come in:

    Start with a knot K : S1 → M and a finite-dimensional representation of the gauge group, ρ. The corresponding Wilson loop operator is defined to be

    Wρ(K) = Trρ[𝒫exp(∫KA)].

    where 𝒫 is the path-ordering operator. The matrix U(K) = 𝒫exp (∫KA) is called the holonomy matrix.

    Now, ⟨Wρ(K)⟩ is a topological invariant.

    Now instead of looking at this correlator, we can instead change our space M, so that it contains a hole / defect — that topological defect is its boundary, and it's precisely the knot K that we want to take the correlator of. We can think of it like a tunnel through the sphere S3.

  2. Jones Polynomial

    In the case G = SU(2) and ρ is the fundamental representation, you get the Jones polynomial skein relation being satisfied by the Wilson loop correlator.

We consider anti-instantons  ⋆ F =  − F.

We find that the moduli space of these anti-instantons is non-compact

Uhlenbeck compactness: Donaldson used this compactification to try to compute integrals on the moduli space.

Supersymmetry: This is actually the high energy limit of N = 2 supersymmetric Yang-Mills.

Seibrg-Witten

We pick a spin structure on a 4-manifold with positive spinor bundle S and determinant line bundle L = det (S). The fields are a U(1) connection on L and a positive spinor ψ ∈ Γ(S).

Turns out, the moduli space is compact

Supersymmetry: This is actually the low energy limit of N = 2 supersymmetric Yang-Mills.

Gromov-Witten

When M is a symplectic 4-manifold, the Seiberg-Witten invariants are equal to the Gromov-Witten invariants obtained from counting pseudo-holomorphic curves.

Ultraviolet Divergence

Generally, in local quantum field theories, we have that the two-point correlator behaves like

$\langle \phi(x) \phi(y) \rangle \sim \dfrac{1}{| x - y|^{d-2}}$

This creates an ultraviolet divergence when we try to bring the points together.

Chern-Simons

We don't have a metric in Chern-Simons thory, so we can actually avoid this UV problem. This allows us to get finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and have extreme locality — this is where the Cobordism Hypothesis comes in

  • because we can chop things up into arbitrarily small points, the full topological quantum field theory ends up being completely determined by what happens on a single point.
