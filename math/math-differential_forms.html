Definitions

Differential forms

Let M be a smooth manifold and take the exterior bundle of the cotangent bundle Λ(T*M). A differential form is a smooth section of the exterior bundle ω : M → Λ(T*M).

A form that lands entirely in ΛkT*M is called a k-form.

Notation: We denote the space of k-forms by ΩkM

Vector-valued differential forms

Let M be a smooth manifold with vector bundle E → M. A differential form is a smooth section of the exterior bundle ω : M → Λ(T*M) ⊗ E.

FAQ

Question: Why don't we just take Λ(E) ?

Answer: That is actually used, but for a different purpose: the goal of a differential form is to act on tangent vectors, which is why we maintain the cotangent part. Taking the exterior bundle directly is used instead for things like characteristic classes

Question: Λ(T*M) is an algebra at each point. So what if we assume E is also an algebra fiberwise?

Answer: This actually shows up in gauge theory! Along with noncommutative geometry and index theory

For example, consider taking End(E), where E is a vector bundle. You get matrix-valued differential forms. This is highly relevant in gauge theories of qft

You can take bundles of clifford algebras, which allow you to define the Dirac operator — this shows up in noncommutative geometry.

If we take E = ℂ, we get complex differential forms, which show up in Kähler and algebraic geometry.

Exterior derivative

Let M be a smooth manifold and ΩkM be its space of k-forms. We define the exterior derivative d : ΩkM → Ωk + 1M by the following axioms

  1. For any f ∈ Ω0M, df is the differential of f.
  2. d(aα+bβ) = ad(α) + bd(β) for all a, b ∈ k and α, β ∈ ΩkM
  3. d(α∧β) = dα ∧ β + (−1)|α|α ∧ dβ
  4. d2α = 0

Coordinates

If we have a local coordinate system xi : U → ℝ, the exterior derivative of ω = ∑fIdxi1 ∧ … ∧ dxin is dω = ∑dfI ∧ dxi1 ∧ … ∧ dxin
