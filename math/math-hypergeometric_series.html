Definitions

Geometric series

A series ∑nanxn is called geometric if there is a constant R such that $$ \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}} = R, \quad \forall n $$

Hypergeometric series

A series ∑nanxn is called hypergeometric if $$ \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}} = \dfrac{A(n)}{B(n)}, \quad \forall n $$

is a rational function in n.

Notes

Assuming we're working over an algebraically closed field, we factor the numerator and denominator as

A(n) = (n − a1)…(n − ap) and B(n) = (n − b1)…(n − bq)

Then since $a_{n+1} = \dfrac{A(n)}{B(n)} a_{n }= \dfrac{A(n)}{B(n)} \ldots \dfrac{A(0)}{B(0)} a_{0} $

We get terms like (n − a1)(n − 1 − a1)…(n − n − a1)

Convention: The standard way to write these things is to factor it as (n + a1)… (so you just take my a1 → −a1) and then write

(a + n)(a + (n − 1))…a = (a)n This is called the Pochhammer symbol.

Then all together we can write our hypergeometric series as

$$ F_{p,q}(\bar{a} , \bar{b}, z) = \sum \dfrac{a_{0} (a_{1})_{k} \ldots (a_{p})_{k}}{b_{0} (b_{1})_{k} \ldots (b_{q})_{k} } z^{k} $$

Convention: Some other conventions are to assume a0, b0 = 1 and that the first non-trivial root of B(n) is b1 = 1, so that (b1)k = (1)k = k! , allowing you to have something like zk/k! , making your series look like an exponential type of thing. In this case, they'll assume you have q + 1 roots on the bottom, so you write

$$ F_{p,q}(\bar{a} , \bar{b}, z) = \sum \dfrac{(a_{1})_{k} \ldots (a_{p})_{k}}{(b_{1})_{k} \ldots (b_{q})_{k} } \dfrac{z^{k}}{k!} $$

Asymptotic series

A series ∑nanxn is called hypergeometric if $$ \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n}} = A(n) $$

is a polynomial function in n.
