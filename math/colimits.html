Definitions

Cones

Given a functor F : 𝒟 → 𝒞, a cone of F is (C, {ϕD}D ∈ 𝒟) such that ϕD : C → F(D) and for all f : D1 → D2, ϕD1f = ϕD2

Limits (cones)

Let F : 𝒟 → 𝒞 be a diagram. A limit of F is a universal cone lim F → F(D)

Limits (Representables)

Let F : 𝒟 → 𝒞 be a diagram. A limit of F is an object lim F such that Hom (A, lim F) ≅ NT (Δ(A), F) is an isomorphism for all A ∈ 𝒞, where Δ is the constant diagram functor, which sends A to the constant functor at A.

In other words, we have a natural isomorphism between presheaves Hom (−, lim F) ≅ NT (Δ(−), F) : 𝒞 → Set

Limits (Constructive)

The limit of a diagram F : 𝒟 → 𝒞 is the equalizer

lim F ≅ eq(∏DF(D) ⇉ ∏f : D → EF(E))

where the first map projects onto the codomain with πE and the second map is F(f)πD

Examples

(Co)equalizers

An equalizer is the limit of the following diagram f, g : A ⇉ B. So it's a universal (E, ϕ) with $E \xrightarrow{\phi} A \rightrightarrows B$.

(Co)products

Products are the limits of discrete diagrams. They have a universal map into all of the factors of the product: πi : ∏iAi → Ai

Initial/terminal objects

The terminal object is the limit of the empty diagram. Because it has no conditions, it has a unique map from every other object into it.

Pullbacks and pushouts

A pullback is the limit of a diagram of the form f1, f2 : A1, A2 → B. So it's the upper left corner of a commuting square.
