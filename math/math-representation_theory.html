Definitions

A representation of a group G is a group homomorphism G → Aut(V) = GL(V) into the automorphisms on a vector space. This is equivalent to saying that it's the structure of a kG-module on a k-vector space V.

  • in other words, G acts on V

A unitary representation is a representation that maps into the unitary operators on a Hilbert spaace G → U(H) We view ℂ G as an involutive structure, with (λ g)* = λ* g-1. Then a unitary representation is a ℂ G module with an inner product such that <g x, y> = <x, g* y> for all g ∈ kG In other words, V is a *-representation.

Inner Product on (kG)*

We can define the inner product as a Frobenius pairing, where we take kG as the coalgebra with duplication coproduct, giving (kG)* an algebra structure. Then we can take the counit to be the average over the group if it's finite

  • for compact groups, you use the Haar measure and take L2(G) instead of the strict dual

  • locally compact groups also have a Haar measure, but the integral over the whole space is infinite You have a few options:

    1. the Plancherel Theorem gives us a measure on the space of irreducible representations (the spectrum of the group), and you integrate over this
    2. you essentially restrict to Schwartz-like functions and use temmpered distributions. The tempered distributions don't have an inner product though. So you have to use a Fourier transform on them to map them back into L2(spectrum)

  • non locally compact groups are difficult:

    • since there's no Haar measure, by definition there's no translation-invariant measure

    • so you use a quasi-invariant measure: actions by the group elements preserve null sets

    • you then define a Radon-Nikodym derivative ρ(g,x) = (x)

    • and define a unitary action on L2(G, μ) with (U(g) ċ f) (x) = f(g-1 x)

    • and you integrate with respect to the quasi-invariant measure

      An example of this is the Wiener measure!

Another solution is to take the colimit of compact subgroups G^• = G1 ≤ G2 ≤ …

Then you take the limit over the the haar measures (when this exists; which is only for certain characters) <φ, ψ> = lim  ∫Gn φ(g) ψ*(g) d μn(g)

Orthogonality

Over ℂ we have that the irreducible characters satisfy

<χi, χj> = ∑g χi(g) χj*(g) = δij

This allows us to decompose an arbitrary complex representation into irreducible pieces using projection

From the kG-module perspective, we have Wedderburn's Structure Theorem

Schur's Lemma

Statement

Let φ1, φ2 be irreducible representations on V1 and V2, and let Ψ: φ1 → φ2 (commutative square) Then

  1. Ψ is an isomorphism or Ψ = 0.
  2. If V1 = V2 and k is algebraically closed then Ψ = λ I for some λ ∈ k.
