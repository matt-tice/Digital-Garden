Summary

Definitions

q-numbers

$$ [n]_{q} = \dfrac{1-q^{n}}{1-q} = 1 + q + \ldots + q^{n-1} $$

q-factorials

$$ [n]_{q}! = [n]_{q} \ldots [1]_{q} = \dfrac{(1-q^{n}) \ldots(1-q)}{(1-q)^n} $$

q-Pochhammer symbol

$$ (a; q)_{n} = \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} (1-aq^{k}) $$

q-Derivative

$$ D_{q} f(x) = \dfrac{f(qx) - f(x)}{(q-1)x} $$

q-Integral

Theorems

Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum groups

$$ [E,F] = \dfrac{K - K^{-1} }{q - q^{-1} } $$

ΔE = E ⊗ 1 + K ⊗ E

$$ \sum (-1)^{k} \binom{n}{k}_{q} E_{i}^{n-k} E_{j} E_{i}^{k} = 0 $$

Rigid deformation: Has the same number of representations, with the same dimensions as the classical case.

Quantum 𝔰𝔩2

We have three generators E, F, H with [H, E] = 2E,  [H, F] = −2F,  [E, F] = H

We try to deform the coproduct on E, F and then apply ΔH = [ΔE, ΔF].

∑E1F1′ ⊗ E2F2′ − F1′E1 ⊗ F2′E2

We want to have a kind of braiding show up when we apply our operators. For instance E(ϕψ) = E(ϕ)ψ + qϕE(ψ)

The motivation is that if instead of thinking about applying E to each of ϕ, ψ, we keep E fixed in the same spot, and swap ϕ, ψ in the second term, picking up the phase factor (thinking of this like the quantum plane, where ϕψ = qψϕ).

But really we should probably pick up q2, because we have to swap ϕ, ψ and then swap ϕ, E(ψ).

Actually, maybe the way to motivate it is to look at the derivative and say you're basically applying the Q-structure from my thesis? Because here now it looks a lot like the graded setting, which is what my Q-bialgebras generalize.
