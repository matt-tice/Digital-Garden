separation axioms

References

Summary

The separation axioms are meant to encode a certain concept of distance on your space. Full metric spaces satisfy all of the separation axioms; as you satisfy fewer and fewer of these axioms, your concept of distance weakens accordingly.

T1 (Tychonoff)

A topological space is T1 if every singleton is a closed set.

T3 (Regular)

A space is T3 if it is T1 and every closed set C and point x ∉ C can be separated by disjoint open sets.

Intuition

The space is locally uniform You get to say that closed sets have sharp edges, and points outside of them have a certain amount of distance.

Examples

  1. Moore-Niemytzki Plane

    Consider ℍ ≥ 0 = {(x,y) ∈ ℝ2 ∣ y ≥ 0}

    For points above the x-axis, open sets are the ordinary open sets in ℝ2. For points on the x-axis, basic open neighbourhoods are open disks that hit the x-axis at exactly that point

    T3 but not T4

  2. Sorgenfrey Product

    ℝℓ × ℝℓ is T3 but not T4.
