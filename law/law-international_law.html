Legalist Paradigm

This is what Michael Walzer called it in Just and Unjust Wars

War is only permissible under 2 conditions:

  1. Defensive against unjust attacker, with the goal of obtaining peace.
  2. Preemptive war in response to a threat that is both overwhelming and imminent.

Alternatively, some states try to argue that we have, as a third category,

  1. Prevention: taking anticipatory action against emerging threats

International Criminal Court

Neither Israel nor the US is a member of the ICC

The ICC only prosecutes individuals. In order to prosecute someone, one of two conditions needs to hold:

  1. The accused is a citizen of an ICC member nation
  2. The crime took place on the territory of an ICC member nation

International Court of Justice

The ICJ handles state-versus-state cases.

Every UN state is party to the ICJ statute. But the ICJ only handles cases in which both parties agree to go to court

Because Israel signed the 1948 Genocide Convention, and Article IX has a clause that says any dispute regarding the treaty must go to the ICJ, they're legally bound to do so.

The ICJ has zero enforcement power

  • the entirety of enforcement is handled by the Security Council, under Article 94 of the UN Charter
  • this allows them to issue global sanctions, asset freezes, arms embargos, or a military intervention or peacekeeping deployment

However, under UN Resolution 377 the General Assembly can use the Uniting for Peace mechanism

  • passing such a UP resolution creates a non-binding recommendation

The most likely outcome of a guilty ruling by the ICJ in the case of a SC veto, is that the guilty nation will suffer severe economic collapse due to corporations and sovereign wealth funds legally and reputationally being required to divest

  • this is what happened to South Africa after apartheid

The ICJ's ruling cannot be appealed

Timeline

Because there's no appeals process, it typically takes 3-7 years for a verdict

Provisional Measures

The Court first adopts provisional measures, to try to limit irreversible harm while the court begins its multi-year process. This is when they decide whether allegations of genocide are plausible

  • if so, they issue an emergency order that legally compels all UN nations that signed the Genocide Convention to prevent genocide

  1. Plausability

    This is an incredibly important topic, because legal plausibility in the context of the ICJ is not plausibility in the colloquial sense. It doesn't mean "likely". And in particular, the ICJ doesn't determine that a crime is plausible.

    • for example, the ICJ can't determine that it's plausible that Israel is committing a genocide
      • which makes sense: how could they possible determine that while in a stage before they're allowed to hear any arguments, or have even established jurisdiction?

    Legal plausibility: This means that the right being claimed by the applicant

    1. exists
    2. is protected under international law

    In the case of Israel, South Africa demonstrated to the ICJ that Palestinians in Gaza have a right to be protected from genocide and South Africa has a right to demand compliance with the Genocide Convention.

Jurisdiction (1-3 years)

The Court first (before looking at any evidence) debates whether it has legal right to hear the case.

The accused is allowed to file Preliminary Objections, arguing that the ICJ doesn't have jurisdiction, or that the filing country has no legal standing, or that proper diplomatic steps weren't followed

  1. Hybrid

    Israel took a hybrid approach, where they decided to combine the jurisdiction and merits phase into one. This speeds things up by years, and risks public sentiment not having enough time to cool down.

Merits (2-4 years)

If they establish jurisdiction, they can start the actual trial.

  1. Memorials

    Several years are spent submitting and going through written evidence (called memorials ), totaling thousands of pages. Each country is given up to a year just to read and respond to the other side's documents

    Then other states are allowed to intervene and submit their own documents.

  2. Intervention

    Third-party nations are allowed to submit formal declarations supporting one side or the other.

    Under Artcle 63 of the ICJ Statute

    • inteventions are not allowed to submit evidence
    • they can only submit a brief abstract legal opinion stating how they believe a specific word of line in the Genocide Convention should be interpreted in general

  3. Hearing

    At the very end, weeks of formal public hearings occur during which the judges question legal teams and examine the evidence

Deliberation

It then takes around 6 months to a year for the judges to deliberate and write the final judgment
