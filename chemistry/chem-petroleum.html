Summary

Petroleum is crude oil (i.e. not refined).

There are a lot of different kinds

Heavy oil has more residuum

Sulfur needs to be removed

Natural gas occurs in the porous rock of the earth's crust, either alone or with petro

When with petro, the gas forms the gas cap, trapped between the petro and the rock

If the pressure is high enough, the gas may dissolve into the petro

Formation

When the petro was forming, it would also release gas In some cases, the gas would remain trapped with the petro In other cases, the gas would be able to escape and migrated to some other rock

Petro Types

Light Oil

Conventional oil Has a small amount of N, O, S

Heavy Oil

More viscous

It's found at a shallower depth

Difficult to extract and transfer

Arises out of biodegradation Bacteria eats up the lighter hydrocarbons

Contains asphaltenes and resins

Lots of NSOs, aromatics, and naphthenes

Saskatchewan

Has lots of heavy oil, but not bitumen

Bitumen / Asphalt

Also called extra heavy oil It has a ton of names

API less than 10

It will actually sink in water, instead of floating on top

It's a liquid, but it's so ridiculously viscous that a single drop will take around 10 years to fall

Carbon emissions from this is 31% higher than conventional oil

Bitumen from oil sands is the most expensive oil to produce

Alberta

Has about 70% of the world's reserves of bitumen

Has 97% of Canada's oil So basically all of Canada's oil is bitumen (in the form of oil sands)

Canada is the only country in the world with large-scale oil sand production

Basically oil sands are only profitable when the price of oil world-wide is very high, so oil sand producers can charge those high prices and recoup costs
