Human Body

Being toxic, ammonia is removed from the body through the urinary system, in the form of dissolved urea in urine

Ammonia is produced in nitrogen fixation

Nitrogen Fixation

A process which converts N2 → NH3

Done by enzymes called nitrogenases.

Nitrogenase

Nitrogen is essential for life. It shows up in a lot of important chemical compounds. But when it does, it's often a single nitrogen bonded to something else.

  • The problem (for living things) with N2 is that its triple bond is ridiculously strong, so living things have a hard time breaking it down into individual nitrogens that they could use.
  • Nitrogenase can break N2 down.

In fact nitrogenases are the only known enzymes that can do nitrogen fixation.

  • So obviously, they're incredibly important for us.

Nitrogenases are produced by certain bacteria, like cyanobacteria and rhizobacteria.
