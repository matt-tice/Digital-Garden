Summary

A deficit is when a government spends more money than it collects through tax in a year.

  • in order to finance this, they issue bonds (the money has to come from somewhere)
  • countries, banks, and people buy the bonds, giving the government money to spend, and in return the government promises to pay back the loan with interest

Alternatively, you could just print more money, but that would increase inflation rapidly

Bond Yields

If a country's debt increases, and they need to issue more bonds to cover the interest, they become forced to sell the bonds with a higher yield

  • in supply and demand you always assume saturation; in this case, that when the previous bonds were issued, people bought their full
    • so when the new bonds come out, if they're the same yield or worse, no one's going to buy them
    • so you have to offer a higher yield, which hasn't become saturated yet

Trickle-Down

Consumer loans and interest rates increase

  • credit cards
  • mortgages
  • car loans
  • business loans

Companies move money into the bonds, which means they have less for expansion and hiring

  • decreases employment
  • eventually drags down GDP and wages

Chicken

Why don't governments play Chicken and fix the price, knowing that if they can't sell their bonds, they won't have the money to pay investors back?

  • governments have far more pressure on them to stay afloat than companies
    • the government would run out of money within days if no one bought the bonds
  • investors are allowed to sell bonds after they buy them, so they can always get some money back if the government tries to play this game

Game Theory

There's some fascinating psychology and math going on here. I'm enthralled by the question of why the government can't just say "here's the price, take it or leave it"

Inflation

If the government tries to print more money to pay off the deficit, they introduce money without changing the amount of goods available

  • because the amount of goods available relative to the amount of cash people have has now decreased, the market increases the price of goods to catch up
  • the currency correspondingly loses value

Debt Spirals

When a company like the US owes trillions due to bonds, the interest it has to pay yearly is gargantuan. If it's too big to cover with taxes, the government has to sell new bonds just to pay the interest on the old ones

Austerity

When the government cuts funding in order to save money, to pay down the debt
