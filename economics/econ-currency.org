* Exchange Rate

** Floating Rate

Exchange rates are determined by market forces.
- Because of this, countries can manipulate their currency's value by buying/selling their reserves

Gives monetary independence and greater flexibility in exchange for lack of stability

Are better at weathering economic shocks and business cycles

** Fixed (Pegged) Rate

Nowadays we have several floating currencies, for major countries, and everyone else is fixed to them

Often a country will maintain the rate of its currency by holding a reserve of its currency --- if the rate gets too high, they sell their currency on the market for some other currency, increasing supply. If the rate is too low, they buy up their currency, reducing supply, by selling their reserves of other currencies

*** Fixing to a Currency

Gives rate stability for specific trading partners
- if you're pegged to the USD, every time you trade with them, your money will be worth the same

Businesses like this and will work with their partner country

However, this comes at the cost of not being in charge of your own currency rate
