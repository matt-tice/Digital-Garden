* Summary

** Nominal GDP

The nominal GDP is the total amount of money in a given economy

** Real GDP

Adjusted for inflation

This gives you something like the total value in a given economy


* Formulas

There are different ways of trying to make GDP mathematically precise


** Income Approach

GDP = COE + GOS + GMI + T - S

COE = Compensation of employees
- wages,  salaries, employer contributions to social security, pensions, etc.

GOS = Gross operating surplus
- profits for corporations
  
GMI = Gross mixed income
- profits for non-incorporated businesses
  
T = Tax

S = Subsidies



** Expenditure Approach

GDP = C + I + G + X - M

C = Consumption
- private expenditures excluding housing

I = Investment
- business investment in equipment, land, etc
- private purchase of housing (both by businesses and consumers)

G = Governmenet Spending
- includes salaries of public servants, purchases of weapons, investment, etc.
- doesn't include social security or unemployment benefits,

X = Exports


M = Imports
- subtracted to avoid counting foreign supply as domestic

* Growth Rate

This is typically more important than the actual value of the GDP. Countries like the US will increase their GDP almost every year, but the question is /by how much?/

So basically you're looking at the acceleration
- the actual GDP is the position
- the increase in GDP per year (the growth) is the velocity
- the growth rate is the acceleration
