financial statements

References

CFA Institute, Investment Foundations, Chapter 7

Summary

Balance Sheet

The balance sheet of a company shows what it owns and how it's financed, including its debts and equity.

Assets

What the company controls. This should be equal to the sum of liabilities and equity

Current Assets

Things expected to be converted to cash or gotten rid of soon — cash, inventory, accounts receivable, etc.

Non-Current Assets

Long-term. Things like land, buildings, machinery, patents.

When you buy a non-current asset the amount paid is reported on the balance sheet under assets, but its cost doesn't necessarily go on the income statement in full

  • you can split up the cost over the whole lifetime of the asset. This is called depreciation, and each year's price is called a depreciation expense

Amortisation is depreciation but for intangible assets.

Liabilities

Short-term/Current Liabilities

Must be repaid in the following year Things like accounts payable, short-term borrowing like loans, Accrued liabilities are unpaid operating expenses or wages

Long-term Debt

Money borrowed that's to be paid off over more than one year

Equity

This is the amount of investment that shareholders have put in by buying stock in the company

Shareholds are residual owners, meaning they own the residual (leftover) value of the company after liabilities are paid.

Stock

Money obtained from selling stock to shareowners

Retained Earnings

Company's undistributed income; essentially what the owners of the company don't take out of the company

Income Statement

Identifies the profit/loss of company. Sometimes called the P&L statement

Revenue

Amount made by the company

Expenses

Cost of cash, inventory, equipment, etc.

Operating Expenses

Cost of goods sold, production, transport, etc.

Operating Income: Revenue - Operating Expenses

This is also called the EBIT (earnings before interest and tax)

EBITDA

Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation.

This is EBIT + D + A

Since D and A are determined through how you do your accounting, EBITDA tells you something about how the company conceptualizes and manages its finances

Interest Expenses

Interest on whatever the company has borrowed

Income Tax

Earnings per Share

Net income = Revenue - (EBIT - I - T)

Earnings per Share = Net income / shares

Cash Flow

Shows cash received and spent. This is not the same as income

  • income is determined as soon as a transaction takes place, even if money hasn't changed hands yet!
    • for example, if products have been shipped to a customer, but the customer hasn't paid yet, it would be in the income statement, but not the cash flow statement
    • similarly, if you pay before the product is shipped, there would be something on the cash flow statement, but not the income statement
    • expenses can show up without having something on the cash flow, by extending credit to the purchaser
    • prepaid things are expenses paid before they occur

Depreciation doesn't show up in the cash flow; instead the cash flow for the long-term asset happens all at once at the time of purchase

CF < 0 and Income > 0, ex. company is slow at collecting cash from customers

CF > 0 and Income < 0, ex. company has high depreciation and amortisation

Companies can operate at a loss (income < 0) but still be fine if the cash flow is positive; income statements are more about how you want to frame things, rather than the actual security of the company

Operating Cash Flow

Cash generated from the company's main operation

Cash inflows: sales Cash outflows: operating expenses

Investing Cash Flow

Cash outflows: purchases of long-term assets like equipment or buildings Cash inflows: sales of long-term assets

Financing Cash Flow

Inflows: raising new capital from shares, borrowing money Outflows: paying dividends, paying off debt, repurchasing shares
