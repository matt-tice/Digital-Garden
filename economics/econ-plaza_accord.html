Summary

In the 1980s, the US was dealing with inflation, and so raised its interest rates

The US dollar ended up appreciating in value as a consequence

  • But this caused american exports to be expensive for other countries
  • So people from other countries didn't really want to buy american products
  • On the other hand, with their strong dollar, americans could import a lot
  • So a trade deficit grew

Presumably, the strong dollar didn't impact purchases domestically But no country can get everything domestically

Similarly, Germany and Japan had a trade surplus due to relatively weaker currencies

Strong Currencies

When a company with a strong dollar does business in a country with a weak dollar, there's an issue: If you do business in another country, it's in terms of their currency. So you'll get all of your profits in terms of a weak currency. If you try to spend it in your home market then, you'll lose a lot of money.

1944

Bretton Woods System

Countries were worried about competitive devaluations

  • By devaluing your own currency, you make it more enticing for other countries to buy your goods
  • You get a race to the bottom

The BW system put everyone's currency in terms of the US dollar, which could always be exchanged at a fixed rate for gold

  • This is called pegging your currency to the dollar
  • This means that the US made a committment that it could, at any time, pay back any amount of USD in gold
    • At this point the US owns half of the world's gold reserves

As time goes on, the US owns less and less gold, and other countries dislike that they have to do everything in USD

  • it basically cost the US nothing to print money, but cost value from other countries (in the form of goods) to acquire USD
  • the perspective of other nations became that they were just there to support Americans' standards of living

1965

French president Charles de Gaulle wants to redeem France's USD for gold. The US panics because they don't have nearly enough

1971

Germany leaves the BW system, after being fed up

  • The USD falls against the Deutschmark

Other countries want out and try to exchange their USD for gold

Congress wants to devalue the dollar in order to prevent other countries from taking all of America's gold
