Language structure

Consonant roots

Like hebrew poetry arabic words are built on three-letter consonant root, allowing connections between words that are unrelated in english

Balagha

The classical science of Arabic rhetoric. One of the really cool things it does is it examines how altering word order changes the emotion of a line

Arabic is an inflected language, allowing a huge variety of word orders

Huge vocabulary

Love, sadness, and the desert all have wide and subtle vocabularies associated to them.

Qasida

A traditional poem

Al-Bihar (the seas)

The 16 classical meters defined by Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi

Syllables: They are either short or long. The 16 classical meters are imagined as seas that the poet swims through

Moving consonants: have vowels

Quiescent consonants: don't have voweels

Bayt (House)

Each line is a bayt, which is self-contained

Halves: Each bayt has two halves: the sadr (chest / first half) and the 'ajuz (rump / second half)

Qafiyah: Every line ends on the same letter and vowel sound

Tafila Poetry (free verse)

Abandoned the bayt and qafiyah

Tafila: A single metrical foot (something like an iamb). It would be repeated and unequal number of times per line
