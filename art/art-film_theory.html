Ontology

What makes film an art?

The Photoplay: A Psychological Study, Hugo Münsterberg (1916)

Silent: This essay was written during the silent film era.

Cinematic techniques, like flashbacks, close-ups, and edits, distinguish it from theatre, and make it a unique artform.

Objectifications: How are viewers able to understand these cinematic techniques? The techniques are really objectifications of mental processes — a close-up objectifies focus

Formalist / Expressionist

Rudolf Arnheim

Cinema is art precisely because it doesn't replicate reality: the physical limitations of the medium force the director to make artistic choices

Talkies: Adding speech to movies actually reduces their artistic merit, diluting the cinematic medium. The silent film is art because it focuses on bodies in motion

Realist

The Ontology of the Photographic Image, André Bazin

The power of cinema is its photographic realism: it preserves time. By having long shots, you allow the viewer to autonomously explore the image, rather than enforcing a singular interpretation

Photography: Because film is based on photography, it has a realist nature

Time: The artistry of film is its ability to present the real world frozen in time

Examples: Bazin loves film makers like Jean Renoir, Orson Welles, and the Italian neo-Realists

Director-as-Author

Criticisms: One of the main issues with auteur theory, is that by focusing solely on the individual, it ignores the broader context of the instutional settings in which these films were made.

Francois Truffaut

Asserted that the director was the auteur — the author of the work. He was against the dominant mode of filmmaking at the time, that simply adapted books to the screen. For Truffaut, in order to create good art, the director needed to have complete control, creating not only the direction, but writing the screenplay as well.

Andrew Sarris

Took up Truffaut's cause in order to legitimize film studies as a discipline. We should focus on the entire ouvre of a given director, not just individual works.

Emotional Engagement

Why do we care about what happens to fictional characters?

Identification: Film theorists often reject the idea that our emotional engagement truly comes from identifying with the characters: we seem to have emotional reactions even to characters we don't identify with.

Simulation

Off-line: Imaging a situation uses the same emotions as if you actually were in it, it's just that you don't act on it.

This is used to explain why we enjoy watching things we'd hate to experience in real life: experiencing an emotion off-line is a pleasurable thing to us.

Criticism: Simulation theory doesn't explain what it means for an emotion to be off-line.

Thought Theory

We can have emotional responses to mere thoughts. The thoughts that we have while watching a film are what trigger our emotional states.

Criticism: why does a mere thought, as opposed only to a belief, evoke an emotional response Beliefs seem stronger than thoughts; more assured. So why is a thought enough?
