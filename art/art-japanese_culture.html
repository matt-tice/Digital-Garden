Environmental

Japan's rapid modernization destroyed a lot of nature and rural settlements in order to build up its industry. We see this reflected in a lot of media that harkens back to a greener era

Pokemon

The creator, Satoshi Tajiri, based the premise on his childhood hobby of catching insects, which he saw die with the rapid urbanization of Japan — people spent less time outside, and animals like insects lost their habitats

Animal crossing

Hikikomori

Isolated protagonists who get transported to another world (isekai) are a direct stand-in for Japan ending its years of isolation under threat by the US during the Meiji era

We can also look at this from an economic perspective: during the 1960s, all the way up to the 80s, Japan had a booming economy. This was achieved by the strict regulation of social roles: men became salarymen, and women, professional housewives. In exchange, they got lifetime employment, steady promotion, and a solid middle-class lifestyle.

After the 1990s bubble burst, Japan culturally realized their insecurity through the economy. Mass layoffs, little hiring, and quality of life recession were huge shocks. This functioned as a broken promise to the people who dedicated 80hour work weeks to upholding the system for decades.

The response was to double-down: anything that strayed outside of the strict social roles was cause for being fired, or not being hired. In order to survive, citizens had to hyper-conform

Personal responsibility: Note that Japanese culture heavily prioritizes the individual's responsibility to the collective; instead of critiquing the system, the natural thing to do was to view not being able to get a job as a personal moral failing. This also goes back to the idea that disaster is natural

Freeters: Those who couldn't get steady employment, and so attempted continual part-time work

Hikikomori: An intense social withdrawl, caused by not being able to meet rigid social standards

Shinto

Izanagi and Izanami

Izanagi: The primordial god in Shinto. His wife is Izanami. When she dies, he goes to the underworld (yomi) to bring her back. Sadly, he finds her body rotting and covered in maggots. He flees, but having touched death, his body is corrupted with kegare

He performs misogi, and as he washes his face, three deities are born:

  • From his left eye, Amaterasu (Sun goddess)
  • From his right eye, Tsukuyomi (Moon god)
  • From his nose, Susanoo (Storm god)

Naruto: This obviously is referenced in Sasuke's powers. The mangekyo sharingan is obtained through witnessing traumatic death, reflecting Izanagi's interaction with his wife's body. In Naruto there are also forbidden eye techniques called Izanagi (which turns the user's death into an illusion at the cost of an eye; used repeatedly by Danzo) and Izanami (trapping the target's mind in an infinite time loop; used on Kabuto)

Orochimaru: Susanoo is exiled from Heaven after destroying Amaterasu's rice fields. He redeems himself by killing the eight-headed snake, Yamata no Orochi using a legendary sword
