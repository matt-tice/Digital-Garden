    Structure: Contains special tissues called aerenchyma. It pulls oxygen from the air, through its leaves, down into the roots. This allows the stalks of the rice to be heavily submerged in water, while the ends poke above the water.

    Climate: Hot, humid

    Water: Massive amount (1200mm annually). Traditionally grown in flooded fields called paddies. Having a flooded field isn't actually necessary, but it's useful at killing competing plants which can't handle being submerged.

  4. Maize

    C4 photosynthesis: An incredibly powerful form of photosynthesis. This allows maize to capture sunlight at an incredible speed, giving more calories per acre than almost any other grain.

    Climate: Warm, sunny

    Water:

    Soil: Rich, nitrogen-heavy. Requires a ton of nutrients

    1. Farming

      Maize doesn't have a tough outer hull, making it easy to harvest and process by hand.

      Nitrogen: Because maize requires a ton of nitrogen to grow, to make sure that it doesn't take all of the nitrogen from the soil, Mesoamerican civilizations invented the Milpa system, which plants beans (which introduce nitrogen back into the soil) alongside maize, and squash (which shade the ground to prevent evaporation)
