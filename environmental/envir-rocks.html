Summary

Rocks are made up of two or more minerals

Types

Igneous

Created from hot molten lava and magma that cools down.

Intrusive igneous rock

Intrusive igneous rock is formed below the earth's surface, from magma.

Magma: Magma is lava found underground. It cools very slowly since it's not accessible to wind. Because of the slow growth period, large crystals have time to form

  1. Granite

Extrusive igneous rock

Lava: Magma that erupts onto the surface of the earth. It cools rapidly, creating fine-grained, glassy textures

  1. Basalt

  2. Obsidian

  3. Pumice

Sedimentary

Formed from tiny pieces of rock, sand, minerals, and dead organisms settle in layers and get squeezed together over millions of years

Fossils: Because they form without extreme heat, they're able to preserve fossils

Sandstone

Shale

Conglomerate

Metamorphic

They start out as igneous, sedimentary, or metamorphic rocks. But then, existing deep inside the earth, the extreme heat and pressure causes them to form entirely new rock!

Dense: The extreme heat and pressure fusing them together makes them very dense

Foliated

Foliated: The pressure will align the minerals into distinct bands/layers

  1. Slate

    Forms from shale

  2. Gneiss

    Forms from granite (intrusive igneous)

Nonfoliated

Minerals recrystalize without forming layers. They have a smooth, uniform structure

  1. Marble

    Formed from limestone

  2. Quartzite

    Formed from sandstone

Rock cycle

Sediment: Wind, water, and ice break down surface rocks like extrusive igneous rocks into sediment, which gets buried and forms sedimentary rock

Metamorphic: Tectonic plates push the layers of the rock deeper, where heat and pressure turns them into metamorphic rock

Igneous: If the rock gets pushed deep enough, it enters the magma and the cycle starts over.
