Summary

The idea behind SMRs is that you build them piecewise, offsite, at factories, and then you can basically just assemble and use them right away, onsite.

It's a kind of fission reactor with a power of  ≤ 300 MWe (megawatt electrical)

There are so far only 2 nations on earth that have SMRs

  1. China
  2. Russia

History

Commercial nuclear power in the US started with the use of small reactor systems in the navy, designed for propulsion

1954

First nuclear powered sub: USS Nautilus (SSN-571) Nuclear propulsion allowed her to stay submerged longer than diesel-electric subs, because she didn't require refueling as often Nuclear subs became the first truly underwater crafts, rather than being surface level crafts that could submerge for short periods

Also nuclear fuel takes up far less space: you can just keep all of it in the reactor

1957

(60MWe) Shippingport plant

1958

First nuclear power aircraft carrier: USS Enterprise (CVN-65) She's the longest naval vessel ever made, though she's now decommissioned

USS Nautilus became first sub to complete a submerged transit of the north pole

1960

USS Triton became the first ship to circumnavigate the globe completely underwater It took 60 days 21 hours

(200 MWe) Dreseden plant

Notes

The 1960s saw a huge increase in nuclear power plants in the US This actually introduced some issues:

  • there was a huge scale-up of plant size within 10 years
  • but not much time had been spent building experience with the smaller plants
  • plants were being built that were 6x the size of the ones operating at the time
  • generally the standard is a max of 2x

This led to rapidly increasing costs and delays, which in turn led to decreased confidence

Another part of the problem was that each new plant was designed to be "one of a kind", increasing costs and complexity

1973 oil crisis leads to a further push for nuclear power But it also led to decreased energy usage by consumers, who couldn't afford high electricity bills
