#+title: flow state
#+filetags: :biology:brain:

* References

* Summary

*Transient Hypofrontality Hypothesis*: During a flow state, your prefrontal cortex actually *deactivates*.
- this allows your inner critic, self-doubt, and self-monitoring to disappear

The [[id:ff008d9b-5b69-4214-bdbb-3cace4500dae][default mode network]] also *turns off* 

* Neurochemicals

Flow states correlate with massive releases of 
- dopamine
- norepinephrine
- endorphins
- anandamide
- serotonin


** Locus Coeruleus

** HCN Channels

Once the HCN channels close, they make it harder to stop flow

This creates an attractor state
- this is the same as ordinary concentration

Somehow the attractor state in flow is more energy efficient than in ordinary focus, which requires reinforcement from the dorsolateral PFC and anterior cingulate cortex

** Anandamide

Induces *transient hypofrontality* 

** Endorphins

Masks the tiredness signals from adenosine.

** Modular Network Topology

During flow, regions required for the task become highly connected with each other, but isolated from the irrelevant regions.

* Brain Waves

In flow states, your waves drop into alpha and theta.

* Conditions

You can trigger flow states by working on challenges that are roughly *4% higher than your current skill level* 

Immediate feedback as to whether what you're doing right or wrong is key

You need a *minimum 15-20min* of uninterrupted concentration
