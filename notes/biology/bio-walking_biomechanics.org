#+title: walking biomechanics
#+filetags: :biology:body:

* References

* Summary

* Body

Walking should be a series of *controlled falls* --- like flying; falling with style.

Your core and pelvis lead, your legs catch you

** Pelvis

Anterior pelvic tilt: sticking your butt out
Posterior pelvic tilt: slouching and tucking your pelvis in

A neutral pelvis allows your hip joints to rotate through their full range of motion, giving you a bit of a butt wiggle

** Feet 

Hit the ground with your heel first and push off with your toes

** Arms

Your opposite leg and arm should be swinging forward together (it's contralateral movement)

Your arms should swing /from the shoulders/

You should get a small twist in your mid-back (thoracic spine) as you walk

* Elegance

Walk from your *lumbar spine and hip flexors* 

Don't try to artificially make your strides longer by stepping further.
- your heel gets jammed into the ground and your walk becomes choppy

Think of your legs going *all the way up to your core and lower back*. 

** Psoas Muscle

Connects to your lumbar spine; goes through your pelvis and attaches at the thigh bone.

** Trailing Leg

The elegance actually comes from the trailing leg

Let your back leg stay straight and extended just a bit longer behind you, opening up your hip flexors

Instead of pulling yourself forward with your front leg, you *push forward with your back leg*
- push from your calf through your ankle and big toe
- you should feel a stretch in the front of your other hip


** Pelvic Rotation

When the right foot steps foward, the right hip rotates forward with it; same with the left

It smooths out your walk
