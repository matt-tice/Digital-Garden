#+title: hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated channels
#+filetags: :biology:brain:hcn:voltage_gated:channel:

* References

* Summary

Located along dendritic spines. Opening the HCN channels causes a *drop in membrane input resistance*.
- input currents leak out of the dendrite before reaching the soma



* Voltage-gated channel

Most channels in the brain open when the cell gets more positive (depolarized). HCN channels open when the cell gets *more negative* --- this is called *hyperpolarization* 

HCN channels are *cation* channels for both *sodium and potassium*. The current made up of these ions is the *I_h current* 
        - sodium tries to enter the cell
        - potassium tries to leave the cell
        - all in all, more sodium comes in than potassium goes out
        - so the cell *gains charge* when the channel is open
        - this helps *reset the cell*, since it started off hyperpolarized (very negative)

* Open

*Elevation of cAMP* causes the channels to open. When this happens, a *leak current flows* across the dendritic membrane

* Closed

To regain focus, you need to close the HCN channels, requiring suppresion of alpha1/beta1 signaling from [[id:2f5d927b-fbd8-4eb2-9c6a-005f78a1c2aa][norepinephrine]], and phosphodiesterases to degrade the cAMP, and then rebuild the original attractor state through the NMDA loops
