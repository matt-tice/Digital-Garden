* Types of Fat

** Brown

Generally considered better for you. Contains *uncoupling protein 1* (UCP1)

Contain iron-rich mitochondria (which is why it's brown)

Concentrated around your neck and collarbones

*** Beige Fat

When you are exposed to cold temperatures, exercise, or fast, some of the white cells *develop mitochondria* and behave like brown fat

*** Cold

You can stimulate BAT with about 2-3 sessions of cold per week (around 4 minutes each)

*** [[id:d6bb9e1b-4d4c-4728-a0a5-656d048fb7ad][Norepinephrine]]

Triggers brown fat

** White

Insulates your body, cushions your organs.

Produces leptin

*** Subcutaneous Fat

*** Visceral Fat

Packed around your abdominal organs

* Resting Metabolic Rate

Surprisingly, your /RMR per pound of muscle/ remains basically the same from age 20 to 60

Your RMR is highly correlated with your muscle mass --- the more muscle you have, the higher your RMR

* Sertraline

Can reduce *non-exercise acivity thermogenesis* (NEAT)
- which is to say, fidgeting, pacing, etc

Does not impact RMR
Does impact your satiation response

* Sedentarity

When muscles sit static for hours, their insulin sensitivity drops

* Muscle

Significantly denser than fat

In order to actually build muscle you need a calorie /surplus/

* NEAT

** Spontaneous Physical Activity

You can intentionally do NEAT movements

*** Soleus Muscle

It runs from the back of the knee to the heel

Highly resistant to fatigue

Rapid heel raises while keeping toes planted and sitting
- rest forearms or weight on your knees


*** Postural Oscillated Shift

Shift your weight from one sit-bone to the other

* Isometric Exercise

Holding a muscle under tension forces the muscle to expend energy rapidly

** Posture

Good posture is a form of isometric exercise
- it uses the *axial stabilizer network*


** Toe Walking

Puts a continuous load on your calves

Leads to Type I (slow-twitch muscle fibers)
- fatigue resistant
- mitochondria-rich
