* Summary

* Circadian Rhythm

Our bodies have two clocks:
1. The SCN
2. The peripheral clocks (individual organs and tissues)

** Suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)

Part of the hypothalmus. Regulates sleep cycles.

Highly sensitive to light and neurological activity

The SCN sends hormonal and temperature signals to the peripheral clocks

The SCN can adjust its sleep-wake schedule within 1-2 days

*** Circadian Alterness Signal

The SCN manages this signal, which fluctuates throughout the day.
This dips around 1-4pm: the *post-prandial dip*

**** Post-Prandial Dip

Most people get sleepy in the early afternoon, between *1pm and 4pm*




** Peripheral Clocks

Adjust their sleep-wake schedule after *5-8 days*

*** Pancreas and Liver

Their clocks determine insulin production
- if they think it's night, even though you're wide awake, and you eat a meal, your pancreas won't secrete enough insulin and your liver will remain in glucose-production

* Core Body Temperature

Reaches minimum 2 hours before you wake

* Sleep Drive

Accumulation of adenosine in the brain builds up every waking hour


* Sleep Types

Sleep clears away adenosine

It also consolidates memories

** Prolactin

In biphasic sleep, in the period between the two sleeps (called *the Watch*), there are high levels of prolactin

During the Watch, avoid using your phone or anything that
1. Triggers unpredictable dopamine (notifications, news updates, social feeds)
2. Suppresses melatonin (blue light)
