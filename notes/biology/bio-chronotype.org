* Circadian Schedules

** Early Bird

Short natural clock cycle (under 24 hours)
- so they naturally want to pull their schedule forward

Steep, high amplitude curve
- intense spike of morning cortisol
- rapid dramaic drop in energy at night

Core temperature minimum at 4am, then increases rapidly

Melatonin produced 2 hours before they fall asleep

** Night Owl

Wider and flatter curves than early birds'

Long natural clock cycle (over 24 hours; 24.3 - 24.5 hours)
- their bodies want to drift later every day

Low amplitude, flatter curve
- cortisol rises slowly and peaks in the late afternoon
- slowly fades at night

Core temperature minimum around 6-7am and rises slowly
- waking up early can leave them deeply groggy

Melatonin produced at slower rate; takes longer for them to sleep after melatonin starts being produced (3-4 hours)

* Free-Running Rhythm

Left to their own devices, night owls would continue to drift and eventually become early birds

** Entrainment

The suprachiasmatic nucleus in your brain resets your internal clock, using external cues called *zeitgebers* 



* Social Jetlag

Because society is based around a 9-5 schedule, night owls typically suffer a kind of social jetlag, because they're forced to work against their body's natural circadian rhythm, leading to chronic sleep debt and circadian disruption.

As a consequence, early birds tend to be a bit more productive in practice
