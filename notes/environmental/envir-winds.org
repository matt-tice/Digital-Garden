* Summary

* The sun

Surprisingly, *winds come from the sun!* More precisely, because hot air rises, in regions where the sun hits, the air gets further away from the earth's surface, creating *low pressure near the surface* and *high pressure higher up*. On the other hand, since cold air sinks, the poles have low-hanging air, creating *high pressure near the surface* and *low pressure higher up*.

=Flow:= Because of these high and low pressure regions, air will flow from the high pressure to the low pressure. So the natural direction for the air to flow is from the equator up into the sky (because of the sun heating it), to the sky around the poles (which are low pressure), down to the surface near the poles (from the cold), down to the surface of the equator (low pressure).

=Rotation:= Because the earth rotates, the natural path of the air is disrupted, in the *Coriolis effect.* The earth *rotates from west to east.* The equator rotates much faster than the poles, causing the distortion of the air currents.

=Distortion:= In the Northern Hemisphere, winds are deflected to their right (to the west), and in the Southern Hemisphere, they are deflected to their left (to the west). This ends up creating three types of winds in each hemisphere: trades (flowing from east to west), westerlies (west to east), and polar easterlies (also east to west)

* Jet stream

An *extremely fast* moving wind high in the atmosphere that acts as a boundary between the cold polar air and warm tropical air.

=Flow:= West to east at around 300 to 400 km/h

=Location:= 30k to 50k feet above the Earth's surface. It's where airplanes fly.

** Cause

The jet stream comes from the *collision of hot and cold air* high up in the atmosphere.

The high air around the equator that rises up wants to head to the polar region with lower pressure in the upper atmosphere. The greater the temperature difference between the equator and the poles, the faster the jet stream travels.

=Rotation:= The Coriolis effect sends the jet stream to the west.

** Types

=Polar jet stream:= The most powerful jet stream. Forms where the polar air meets the mid-latitude air. There's a very large temperature differential between these two airs.

=Subtropical jet stream:= Forms around 30 degrees latitude, where mid-latitude air meets hot equatorial air. Sits higher up in the atmosphere, but is weaker, because the temperature contrast isn't as drastic.

** Arctic amplification

Because the Arctic is warming *four times faster than the rest of the globe* the temperature differential between the equator and poles is shrinking, which in turn weakens the flow from the equator to the poles.

=Rossby waves:= When the temperature differential weakens, the Jet Stream slows 

** Wind slowing

When the temperature differential weakens and the speed of the wind movement from the equator to the poles slows, it causes weather events to *prolong*

* Tropical cyclones

Massive, rotating, low-pressure weather systems.

=Vetical:= Hurricanes are *very vertical*; they're basically straight up and down.

=Heat:= It's unimaginable, how much heat a hurricane generates. A single mature hurricane creates *200 times the entire world's electrical generating capacity* in heat energy

** Causes

Like tornadoes, these are caused by warm moist air (in this case, from the ocean) meeting cold air. 

As the warm air rises, it creates a low pressure region, drawing in more air

=Deep warm water:= In order to form a cyclone, ocean water much reach a surface temperature of at least 26.5C, down to a depth of about 50m. It's this massive well of warm water that creates the cyclone.

=Disturbance:= Cyclones are usually formed within a cluster of thunderstorms or a tropical wave (an area of low pressure moving through the tropics).

=Moist unstable air:= With high humidity in the lower and middle layers of the atmosphere, rising air is able to continue to grow as it travels upwards, without drying out

=Low vertical wind shear:=  Unlike tornadoes, which rely on wind shear, cyclones will be destroyed by strong crosswinds. This is for a few reasons: the top of the storm clouds can get blown apart; the latent heat released inside the hurricane can be blown away, causing the air to start to cool and fall back down; dry air can get shoved into the cyclone causing rain to evaporate, *cooling* the air and killing the warm updrafts.

=Coriolis effect:= To form, cyclones need to utilize the earth's spin. The Coriolis effect is too weak near the equator, so cyclones usually form at least five degrees latitude away from the equator.

** Formation

Heat from the ocean causes water to evaporate, creating low warm, moist air. As this air rises, more air comes in to take its place, which also gets warmed by the ocean, continuing the cycle. 

=Condensation:= As the air rises and cools, it condenses, releasing latent heat, causing it to warm and propel itself upwards even faster.

=Rotation:= The rotation of the cyclone is caused by earth's rotation

=Cold water:= Since the cyclone needs warm water to heat surface air to sustain itself, it will dissipate once it reaches cooler water

** Terminology

=Hurricanes:= In the North Atlantic and Northeast Pacific. These spin *counterclockwise* 

=Typhoons:= Northwest Pacific. Spin *counterclockwise.* 

=Cyclones:= South Pacific and Indian Ocean. Since they're in the southern hemisphere, these spin *clockwise* 

* Tornadoes

Small, localized, intensely violent column of rotating air.

=Size:= Much smaller than tropical cyclones, usually only touch the ground for a few minutes. However, because they're smaller, they rotate much faster --- reaching *300mph*, leading to extreme localized damage.


** Causes

=Thunderstorms:= Tornadoes typically form inside of severe thunderstorms, like supercells. 

=Temperature:= When warm humid air collides with cold dry air, the warm air is forced upwards, as the cold air travels downwards. The rapid movement of warm air upwards is called an *updraft.* As the updraft cools, it creates storm clouds. 

=Wind shear:= Wind shear is when the wind speed and direction changes at different heights. Winds near the surface of the earth are usually blowing in a different direction and at a different speed than those higher in the atmosphere. The wind shear causes air to *roll horizontally* between the different directions and speeds

=Updraft:= The powerful spurt of air travelling upwards catches the rolling tube of air and pushes it *vertical* --- this causes the entire column of rising air to *start to spin*, forming a *mesocyclone.* 

=Angular momentum:= As air rushes higher, the spinning column gets stretched vertically, and starts to *spin faster*, by conservation of angular momentum. 

=Touching down:= The cold sinking air forms a *downdraft*, which pushes the vortex downwards until it reaches the ground, becoming a *tornado.* 

* Prevailing winds

Winds that blow predominantly in one specific direction.

** Trade winds (easterlies)

The trade winds are *east-to-west* blowing winds that flow in the equatorial region

=Colonialism:= These are the winds that captains used to sail to the Americas.

** Westerlies

These winds blow *west-to-east*. They sit in the *middle latitudes* (between 30 and 60 degress latitude)

=Specific heat capacity:= The inertia of heat --- it tells you how easily it is to change the temperature of a substance. A low specific heat capacity means that the material will heat up and cool down quickly. 

Land has a low specific heat capacity, while water has a high specific heat capacity.

=Seasons:= In the summer, *large landmasses* will heat up quickly, while the surrounding water stays cool. In contrast, in the winter, the land cools rapidly, while the ocean is relatively warm

=Pressure:= In the summer, the hot air over the land rises, creating a low pressure region, which the cool, low air of the ocean moves into. This air carries huge amounts of water from the ocean, and carries it over the land. 

=Mountains:= If there are [[id:e3b0765d-adcd-4e70-90e0-9bf80ef62654][mountains]], orographic rainfall will be produced from the ocean air being driven higher and cooling. This creates a *monsoon* 

=Landmass:= It's important to note that three pieces are all necessary to create monsoons: a large body of water, a large landmass, and mountains. Having a small landmass, like an archipelago actually prevents monsoons
