#+title: problem of induction
#+filetags: :math:philosophy:

* References



* Summary

The problem of induction is the following: /how do we logically justify that knowing things about the past informs us about the future?/ 

This is obviously an important problem in the philosophy of science.

* Hume

Introduced the problem of induction. He viewed inductive reasoning as circular: the only reason we use it, is because it /worked in the past./ But using taking that leap from it being useful in the past to being useful subsequently is precisely the problem of induction in the first place.

=Deduction:= We can't prove induction deductively, because there's nothing inherently contradictory in the inductive reasoning failing to hold.

=Observation:= We also can't prove induction empirically, because that brings us to the circular argument: to jump from empirical observation to a conclusion requires induction to hold.
