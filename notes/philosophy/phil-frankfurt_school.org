* Summary

A collection of philosophers and social scientists associated with the *Institut für Sozialforschung* (Institute for Social Research)


* Members

** Max Horkheimer

Developed the field of *critical theory*, which he called *materialism*. It's highly based off of /Marxism/
        - social philosophy, for Horkheimer needs to focus on *alleviating suffering*


*** Hegel

Horkheimer doesn't like Hegel. Horkheimer claims Hegel /transfigued oppression/, by subjugating suffering to reason: individual human experience is only understood within the rational progression of the /eternal life of Spirit/ 
        - this separates thinkers from the reality of human suffering

At the same time, Horkeimer is critical of social scientists that don't look at society /as a whole./ 

The goal of materialist research is to solve both of these problems. It should elucidate the links between economics, psychology, and culture, to provide a *unified theory of society* that can replace those of Universal Reason or Spirit



He and Adorno (his teacher) wrote *Dialectic of Enlightenment* 

*** Eclipse of Reason (1947)

This work is about *how reason collapses into irrationality* through *instrumental concerns*

=Instrumental reason:= Its purpose is to achieve certain ends, without reasoning /about those ends/ 

=Positivism:= All aspects of nature that are incalcuable are ignored by Enlightenment thinking 

=Subjective reason:= Is considered to be the *same as instrumental reason*. Subjective reason is oriented towards the end of self-preservation, and indeed all instrumental reason is really towards that end. This leads to *contradiction* --- because subjectivity only cares about self-preservation, it eventually even forgoes an understanding of the significance of what it even means to be alive in the first place

=Objective reason:= The metaphysical systems of Plato, Hegel, etc. It seeks to *center truth and meaning*. These are the /proper ends of thought./ 

*** Dialectic of Enlightenment

=Enlightenment:= It functioned as the *advancement of instrumental reason*; and through this process, it grew to be *in conflict with itself* 

=Formal logic:= Considered a kind of instrumental reason. For Adorno and Horkheimer, the the goal of formalism is to make nature calculable

=Cliques:= Self-preservation forms an end; instrumental reason geared towards this end leads to forming cliques

=Culture:= Instrumental rationality has taken culture and made it a part of an industrial model, leading to *homogenization*. This only way to survive is to become incorporated into it

*** Karl Marx

Adorno reads Marx as a *Hegelian materialist* whose critique of capitalism *includes a critique of the ideologies that capitalism requires* 

=Culture:= Orthodox Marxism views culture as part of the *superstructure*, therefore determined by (and reflects) the economic base

**** Culture industry

This is Adorno's idea based off of Marx. It looks at how *entertainment functions in mass society* 

Entertainment exists in a /cycle of manupulation and retroactive need/ 

=Commodities:= Mass cultural products function as commodities, and thereby *reinforce empirical social reality*; they /[idolize] the existing order/ 

*** Theses on the Language of the Philosopher

Adorno rejects positivist (what he called "idealist") theories that attempt to *distinguish linguistic form from its content* and treat things as /functions of thought/. 
        - these theories take /reason/ as the primary architect of art
        - thereby, they treat linguistic signification as *arbitrary*
        - what can be known in these theories is precisely what can be communicated through language


**** Heidegger

Adorno also rejects Heidegger's approach for straying too far into abstraction, and not looking at how concepts get concretized in history and words.

**** Adorno's approach

/Through language history wins a share of truth/ 
/History and truth meet in the word/ 

=Linguistic reality:= The *name* of a thing is a unity of form and content; it unifies material reality and linguistic expression into a single concept. Adorno calls this unity *configurative language*: /a dialectically intertwined and explicatively indissoluable unity of concept and thing./ 

=Double character:= Language has two different parts:

        1. =Communicability:= Conceptualist, representational, significatory

        2. =Expression:= (Ausdruck) Non-conceptual, evocative, gestural

*** Dialectic of Enlightenment (1939 - 1944)

Written by Adorno and Horkheimer while in exile in California. It's a critique of reason, society, and the self in Western modernity. Adorno considered this his *main work* 

=Enlightment:= Is interpretted as a liberatory philosophy, meant to free humans from fear and make them "masters". The irony is that the world is awash in violence and oppression. 

=Reason:= The history of reason in western thought has led to /a new kind of barbarism/; an ironic twist from its intention. The purpose of /Dialectic of Enlightenment/ is to explain *how this happened* 

=Capitalism:= Its calculation is based on enlightenment ideas. It seems as if /enlightenment itself is totalitarian/ 

=Myth:= Hegel's historiography held myth and englightenment to be two *mutually exclusive* concepts, which governd successive stages in humanity's development. Adorno and Horkheimer argue that myth and enlightenment are actually inseparable; each one reinforces the other
        - in order to tame myth, religion and ritual develop, but this introduces the concepts of abstraction, representation, exchange
        - but then the abstract concepts of enlightenment themselves become deified, forming the basis of a new myth
        - the irony is clear: even as enlightenment's scientific thinking places itself above myth, it is myth itself

        =Art:= Art and science end up in conflict. Language, being a descriptive tool, cannot represent anything, while art can only represent by *renouncing knowledge* of its subject

        But when humans, in pursuit of science, cut themselves off from nature, the purpose for which they live falls away, and the *means becomes the end* 

**** Technology

Technology moves to isolate an individual from their consciousness.
        - the telephone allowed the user to be the subject, to play a role as a participant
        - the radio turned *all participants into listeners* underneath an authoritarian broadcast

Amateur filmakers, broadcasters, etc. are admonished as /amateur/, and their work is controlled by talent scouts, studio competitions, etc. that are curated by the establishment

**** The Odyssey

The Grundtext (fundamental text) of European civilization

Odysseus is the *prototype of the bourgeois individual*. He represents the failed attempt to escape myth
        - he only gets past the sirens by making himself /thing-like/, without hearing, being tied to a mast


**** Suspension of the Dialectic

Adorno and Horkheimer view the dialectic between myth and enlightment to be at a point of *aporia*; it recognizes its own controdiction

=Determinate negation:= (Bestimmte Negation)
You rethink the assumptions. For Adorno, this means rethinking the material, societal conditions in which we live.

=Conceptual self-reflection:= (Der Begriff als Selbstbesinnung des Denkens)

To overcome the dialectic, enlightenment has to *reflect on itself* 

**** Habermas

Created an influential line of criticism against Adorno and Horkheimer's work: why should we conflate instrumental rationality and rationality tout court? why should we conflate rationality with power?

=Normative deficit:= Adorno and Horkheimer's work tries to attack a totalitarian nature in Enlightment by using none other than the tools of the Enlightenment itself! It is /a critique that attacks the presuppositions of its own validity/. It is therefore imposible for them to practice true determinate negation; they are merely negating on an *ad hoc basis* 

*** Music

=Culture industry:= Popular music existed as part of the culture industry; this music was mass-produced, formulaic, standardized and designed to make money. It offers an *illusion of choice* while *enforcing social conformity* 

**** Jazz

Adorno *hated jazz*. For him, jazz was the canonical masquerade; it presented itself as a way of escaping from rigidity, but inevitably was bound by the underlying rhythm.

With that basis, it's hard to imagine Adorno liked /any/ music. But he did --- he liked *Schoenberg's atonality*, which he viewed as intentionally denying the temptation of commercialization

**** Bach and Mozart

Suprisingly, Adorno also liked people like Bach and Mozart. This seems tremendously odd, considering that Bach's entire ouvre was designed as a way to pay the bills while *reinforcing the cultural standards of the Lutheran church*, and therefore seems to pretty conclusively be part of the culture industry.

But Adorno says no: to him, Bach's music was itself rebelling against the confines of the time [ /Bach Defended Against His Devotees/ ]. He viewed Bach's counterpoint and chromaticism as a precursor to the atonality of the modern period.

Again, this is quite odd, considering that Bach's work was /literally formulaic/, in that he followed rigid structures in order to pump out the massive volume of pieces he did. He continually reused melodies, he opted for the most basic lyrics possible, etc.

And, again, Adorno was unable to see any degree of rebellion, or wrestling against material confines in the work of jazz artists, even though he saw that they intentionally were railing against the supposedly confining structure of the beat

=Unity:= Bach's work employed each note for the purposes of internal consistency; its end was not eliciting a particular emotion from the audience
