* Summary

Under each scale, I'll write down each of the corresponding modes

But I'll also write down a table for the different possibilities of flattening or sharpening notes

* Terminology

I'll use the following convention:

=Major:= Natural 3

=Minor:= b3

=Dominant:= b7

=Harmonic:= Natural 7

* Table

It seems like we can flatten any of the degrees 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, without any trouble. Flattening the fourth degree is a bit more subtle, since if you don't also flatten the third, the fourth and third collapse into the same note.

There are quite a few combinations, then. I guess $2^{5} = 32$, just for flattening. So, for the sake of formatting, instead of having one large table, I'll probably split it up into multiple smaller tables

* Major Scale

=Ionian:= Major scale

=Dorian:= Minor scale with a raised 6 (flat 3, 7)

=Phrygian:= Minor scale with b2 (flat 2, 3, 6, 7)

=Lydian:= Major scale with #4

=Mixolydian:= Major scale with b7

=Aeolian:= Minor scale (flat 3, 6, 7)

=Locrian:= Minor scale with flat 2 and flat 5 (flat 2, 3, 5, 6, 7)

* Harmonic Minor Scale

=Harmonic:= Flat 3, 6

=Locrian sharp 6:= Flat 2, 3, 5, 7

=Ionian sharp 5:=  #5

=Dorian sharp 4:= Flat 3, 7 and #4

=Phrygian dominant:= Flat 2, 6, 7

=Lydian sharp 2:= Sharp 2, 4

=Ultralocrian:= Flat 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, bb7

* Melodic Minor Scale

=Lydian augmented:= Sharp 4, 5

=Lydian dominant:= Sharp 4, b7

=Melodic major:= Flat 6, 7

=Locrian 2:= Flat 3, 5, 6, 7

=Altered:= Flat 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7
