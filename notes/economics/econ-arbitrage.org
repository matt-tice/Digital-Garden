#+title: arbitrage
#+filetags: :economics:stocks:market:

* References

* Summary

* Pure Arbitrage

When an asset is priced differently on two different exchanges

You buy asset X on one exchange for $110 and immediately sell it on a different exchange for $120

* Triangular Arbitrage

Convert currencies A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow A

* Convetible Arbitrage

[[id:818015c0-1bce-4282-b6dd-53cef3412d20][Institutional investors]] take long positions in a company on private placements, while simultaneously shorting the company

* Statistical Arbitrage

Looks for two or more highly correlated assets { A_i } that have *cointegration* --- a linear combination of their prices forms a stationary time series around a constant mean

The arbitrage technique will short the assets above the mean and go long in the ones below

* Volatility Arbitrage

=Delta-neutral hedging:= Constructing a portfolio where the net volatility is zero

* Capital Structure Arbitrage

Companies have both equity and debt. *Merton's model* views equity as a call option on assets, where the strike price is the value of its liabilities

A stock price of a company can change sooner than its bonds reflect the news, or vice versa.

* Index Arbitrage

Sometimes an index actually trades slightly above or below the atual weighted market value of the basket of stocks it's supposed to track
