* Summary

Physical commodity market

Leveraged financial derivative


Something like HOU is designed to deliver 200% of the *daily return* of crude oil futures.
- it is decidedly /not/ meant to track the /price of oil/ over time
- the fund resets its leverage every day

** Volatility Decay


** Futures

HOU holds near-month crude oil futures contracts

Futures for oil are often in *contango*

*** Contango

The price for delivery in the future is /higher/ than the current (spot) price.

** Liquidity

Because HOU is highly liquid, when things go bad for hedge funds, and they need liquidity fast, a fund like HOU is one of the first things to sell, massively crashing the price

* Derivatives

Derivatives are contracts whose value is derived from the price action of a commodity

Derivatives are zero-sum

They're the first to go in volatile times
- they get hit quickly by margin calls, because they allow high leverage

** Math

If P is the price of the underlying asset, the derivative price is
V(P,t)
\partial V/ \partial P  = \Delta  is the "delta"

\partial^2_P V is gamma.

High gamma means serious price spikes or crashes

\partial_t V is theta
- it tells you about how the derivative loses value, even if the price of the underlying commodity is fixed

Alpha is the return that isn't explained by the market
- it's the actual skill of the trader

Beta is a measure of how your system correlates with the market
-  \beta = 2 means that you're amplifying the market's movement by 200%
