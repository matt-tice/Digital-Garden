** Dualizable object in a monoidal category

An object in a monoidal category \(A \in \mathcal{C}\) is *right dualizable* if there is an object \(A^{*}\) and morphisms
\[
\varepsilon_{A}: A^{*} \otimes A \rightarrow 1 \quad \eta_{A}: 1 \rightarrow A \otimes  A^{*}
\]
such that 
\[
\eta_{A} \otimes 1_{A^{*}}\circ \alpha \circ 1_{A^{*}} \otimes \varepsilon_{A} = \lambda^{-1}_{A^{*}} \circ \rho_{A^{*}}
\]
and 
\[
1_{A} \otimes \eta_{A}\circ \alpha \circ \varepsilon_{A } \otimes  1_{A} = \rho^{-1}_{A} \circ \lambda_{A}
\]

=Invertible:= In the case that *\varepsilon, \eta* are isomorphisms, we call \(A\) an *invertible object.* 

*** Terminology

Other people will call this a /left/ dual. I'm using right dual to signify that the unit-like map \(\eta: 1 \rightarrow A \otimes A^{*} \) puts the dual on the right of the tensor product, with the intuition that a unit should be something like the existence of the object.

** Rigid monoidal category

A category \(\mathcal{C}\) is *right rigid* if every object has a right dual. Similarly it is left rigid if every object has a left dual. A *rigid* category is one that is both left and right rigid.

** Simple object in a category with terminal objects

Let \(\mathcal{C}\) be a category with a terminal object \(\bullet.\) An object \(A\) is *simple* if every coequalizer of the form
\[
E \rightrightarrows A \rightarrow Q
\]
has \(Q \cong 1\) or \(Q \cong A\)

** Semisimple cat

An abelian category \(\mathcal{C}\) is *semisimple,* if every object is a direct sum of finitely many simple objects.

** Fusion category

A *fusion category* \(C\) is a rigid, semisimple, Vect-enriched monoidal category, with only finitely many  simple objects (up to isomorphism), and such that the endomorphisms of the unit object just form the ground field \(k.\)

*** Intuition

The goal of a fusion category is to study how objects combine

Since every object can be written as a direct sum of finitely many simple objects, and there are only finitely many simple objects, \(X_{i}\), we have a kind of *structure coefficient* equivalence that we can write:

\[
X_{i} \otimes X_{j} \cong \bigoplus_{k} N_{ij}^{k} X_{k}.
\]
where \(N_{ij}^k \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\).

** Ribbon category

A *ribbon category* is a rigid braided monoidal category \((\mathcal{B}, \otimes , \sigma )\) along with a natural isomorphism \(\theta: I \rightarrow I\) from the identity functor to itself, called a *twist* such that 

\[
\theta_{A \otimes B} = \sigma_{BA} \circ \sigma_{AB} \circ \theta_{A} \otimes \theta_{B}
\]
\(\theta_{1} = id\), where \(1\) is the monoidal unit
\(\theta_{A^{*}} = \theta_{A}^{*}\)

*** Intuition

These categories look at how things knot and twist together

=Twist:= The twist operation is meant to account for the *spin* of a particle. As it turns out, the world lines of anyons shouldn't really be thought of as world /lines/, but rather world /ribbons,/ where the extra dimension corresponds to the spin

=Quantum groups:= Finite-dimensional representations of a quantum group form ribbon categories.

** Modular tensor category

A *modular tensor category* is a ribbon and fusion category \(\mathcal{C}\) which satisfies a non-degeneracy condition

*** Intuition

These are similar to ribbon categories, but prevent certain kinds of singularities that can show up in the knots

Let \(\{ X_{i} \}\) be a set of representatives of the simple objects.

=Trace:= Let \(f: A \rightarrow A\) be an endomorphism. We define the trace of \(f\) by

\[
Tr(f) = \varepsilon \circ (id_{A^{*}} \otimes  (f \circ \theta_{A}) ) \circ (id_{A} \otimes  \theta_{A^{*}}) \circ \sigma_{A^{*}A} \circ \eta
\]

It's similar to the ordinary trace, but we have a twisting action on both strands. Note that \(Tr(f) : 1 \rightarrow 1\) is a member of the endomorphism ring of the vacuum, which by the definition of a fusion category, is isomorphic to the ground field. So \(Tr(f) \in k.\)

In the case that your category is the standard category of vector spaces, you get back the ordinary trace (because the twist is trivial there).

=S-matrix:= To form the Smatrix, we take the trace of a double braiding:

\[
S_{ij} = Tr(\sigma_{A_{j} A_{i}} \circ \sigma_{A_{i} A_{j}} )
\]

=Non-degeneracy:= The non-degeneracy condition is equivalent to the Smatrix being non-degenerate.

=TQFTs:= Modular tensor categories describe \(2+1\) dimensional topological quantum field theory.
