* Summary

If a chain complex encodes the homotopical nature of spaces, the algebraic structure on top, turning it into a dga, encodes the geometry / coordinate functions.

In the de Rham complex, the higher chains encode higher derivatives.

* Definitions

** Differential graded algebra

A differential graded algebra is a monoid in the category of chain complexes of vector spaces.

Concretely, the tensor product in chain complexes has
\[
(A \otimes B)_{n} = \bigoplus_{i+j=n} A_{i} \otimes B_{j}
\]
and \( d_{n} = \sum_{i+j = n} d_{A_{i}} \otimes 1_{B_{j}} + (-1)^{i} 1_{A_{i}} \otimes d_{B_{j}} \)

So a dga would have a multiplication
\[
\mu: A \otimes A \rightarrow A
\]
which acts like 
\[
d_{A, n} \circ \mu_{n} = \mu_{n-1} \circ  d_{A \otimes A, n}
\]

Unpacking this, we have for all \( x,y \in A \) with \( |x| = i, |y| = j \), 
\[
d_{A,i+j}(xy) = d_{A}(x) y + (-1)^{i} x d_{A}(y)
\]

** Commutative dga

A dga is called commutative if for all homogeneous  \( x,y \) we have 
\[
xy = (-1)^{|x||y|} yx
\]

* Examples

** Chain complexes

Any chain complex can be given the structure of a dga with \( xy = 0 \) for all \( x,y. \)

** Graded algebras

Any graded algebra can be viewed as a dga with \( d = 0 \)

** de-Rham dga

Let \( M \) be a manifold and \( \Omega^{\bullet} (M) \)  be its de Rham cochain complex of differentials. This forms a *commutative dga*, with the ordinary multiplication of the exterior products

** Singular cochains

Let \( X \) be a topological space and consider the singular cochain complex with coefficients in a ring \( R \)
\[
C^{\bullet} (X ; R)
\]

=Cup product:= 

This is not a commutative dga in general. However in the case that \( X \) is a smooth manifold and \( R = \mathbb{R} \), we have that the singular cohomology matches the de Rham cohomology and the cup product matches the exterior product --- thus in that case we do get that our dga is commutative

** Free dga

Let \( V \) be any non-graded vector space. The free dga is formed by taking the tensor algebra \( TV = \bigoplus_{n} V^{\otimes n} \), and viewing it as a chain complex with a differential defined by any linear map \( f: V \rightarrow k \)

\[
d_{f}(x_{1} \ldots x_{n}) = \sum_{i} (-1)^{i-1} v_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes f(v_{i}) \otimes \ldots \otimes v_{n}
\]
