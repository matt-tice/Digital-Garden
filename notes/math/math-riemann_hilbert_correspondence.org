* Summary

=Idea:= There's a correspondence between systems of differential equations and certain kinds of sheaves

=Monodromy:= When you have singularities, analytic continuation becomes difficult --- you try to spread outward from your starting region, but if you pass around a singularity and get back to your starting region, you can wind up with a different function than the one you started with. Monodromy is essentially a way of capturing how much distortion happens in analytic continuation

=21st problem:= Hilbert's 21st problem was about the existence of differential equations with certain singularities and monodromy. Basically he wanted to know, for any choice of singularities and monodromic group, is there always a linear differential equation (of Fuchsian type) with those features?

=Dmodules:= In one dimension, you can approach differential equations using vector bundles with flat connections --- this was Deligne's approach. But in higher dimensions, the singularities become complicated, since they're no longer isolated points, but rather surfaces/subvarieties. To deal with this, you look at Dmodules, in the form of sheafs of differential algebras. Sheaves work with singularities more naturally

=Correspondence:= The correspondence that ends up working is between regular holonomic Dmodules (corresponding to finite-dimensional solution spaces, and mild singularities) and perverse sheaves (some kind of chain complex of sheaves)

=Poincaré duality:= Ordinary smooth manifolds have Poincaré duality. But once you introduce singularities, this breaks. To fix it, you need *perverse sheaves* 

=Stratification:= To build a perverse sheaf, first you perform a stratification of your space, which slices it into smooth pieces of decreasing dimension. You then take a chain complex of sheaves whose cohomology is constant on each stratum

=Intuition:= The stratification is a process of picking out singularities. The condition of constancy (called *constructability*) basically says that your singularities are fairly nice. The reason you want the constructability is so that the only bad behaviour comes from *transitioning between strata*, not from moving within a fixed stratum. The stratification corresponds (in the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence) to the fact that the Dmodule differential equations are holonomic (finite solution space)

=Example:= Consider \( \mathbb{C} \) --- we stratify it with a single point (the origin), and the punctured plane.

=Flat connections:= Every flat connection actually corresponds to a Dmodule --- a flat connection on \( E \rightarrow M \) turns \( E \) into a Dmodule (a space on which differential operators, or more precisely a sheaf of differential operators, acts). Locally constant sheafs correspond (by the classical Riemann-Hilbert correspondence) to vector bundles with flat connections! Basically this means that you don't do the stratification procedure (so you just have a single locally constant sheaf); meaning you don't have singularities (which is why you get a flat connection)
