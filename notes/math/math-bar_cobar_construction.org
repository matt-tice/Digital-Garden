#+title: bar-cobar construction
#+filetags: :math:homotopy:

* References


* Summary

=Bar:= The bar construction takes a weak algebraic structure (like a homotopical structure) and replaces it by a strict (non-homotopy) structure, by first considering all formal compositions, and then quotienting out by certain relations.

It's called the bar construction, because you write a formal composition of algebraic elements using bars:
\[
[x_{1} \mid x_{2} \mid \ldots \mid x_{n}]
\].
These formal compositions are then interpretted as *simplices.* This allows you to turn *algebraic structure into spaces* 

=Cobar:= Breaks things into *formal decompositions*. This is very useful with quasicategories: there is no single composition of \(1\)-morphisms --- given a \(2\)-simplex, it's really a collection of two different paths that are homoptic (as witnessed by the \(2\)-simplex). The cobar construction then breaks this simplex down into a sum of the different constituent \(1\)-simplices. This allows you to turn *spaces into coalgebraic structure* 


Uses differential graded (co)operads to introduce homotopy.

Two steps:
1. Bar: a projection
2. Cobar: a free reconstruction

** Requirements

You need to be able to dualize

You need an augmented operad

You typically work with linear operads

You typically assume a connected operad

* Bar Construction

Takes an operad and constructs a cooperad

More generally it takes a monad algebra and turns it into a simplicial object
