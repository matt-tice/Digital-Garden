#+title: bar-cobar construction
#+filetags: :math:homotopy:

* References


* Summary

=Bar:= The bar construction takes a weak algebraic structure (like a homotopical structure) and replaces it by a strict (non-homotopy) structure, by first considering all formal compositions, and then quotienting out by certain relations.

It's called the bar construction, because you write a formal composition of algebraic elements using bars:
\[
[x_{1} \mid x_{2} \mid \ldots \mid x_{n}]
\].
These formal compositions are then interpretted as *simplices.* This allows you to turn *algebraic structure into spaces* 

=Cobar:= Breaks things into *formal decompositions*. This is very useful with quasicategories: there is no single composition of \(1\)-morphisms --- given a \(2\)-simplex, it's really a collection of two different paths that are homoptic (as witnessed by the \(2\)-simplex). The cobar construction then breaks this simplex down into a sum of the different constituent \(1\)-simplices. This allows you to turn *spaces into coalgebraic structure* 


Uses differential graded (co)operads to introduce homotopy.

Two steps:
1. Bar: a projection
2. Cobar: a free reconstruction

** Requirements

You need to be able to dualize

You need an augmented operad

You typically work with linear operads

You typically assume a connected operad

* Definitions

** Augmentation

A linear functional \( \varepsilon : A \rightarrow k \) on an algebra is called an *augmentation* if 
\[
\varepsilon \circ \eta = 1_{k}
\]

The pair \( (A, \varepsilon ) \) is called an *augmented algebra*. 

The *augmentation ideal* is \( \ker \varepsilon  \), and we have 
\[
A \cong k \oplus \ker \varepsilon 
\]


** Coaugmentation

Let \( C \) be a coalgebra. A linear map \( \eta: C \rightarrow k \) is a *coaugmentation* if 
\[
\varepsilon \circ \eta = 1_{k}
\]

The *coaugmented coideal* \( \bar{C} \) is the coalgebra \( \operatorname{coker} \eta   \), and 
\[
C \cong k \oplus \operatorname{coker} \eta 
\]

** Reduced comultiplication

Let \( (C, \varepsilon ) \)  be a coalgebra with coaugmentation \( \eta : k \rightarrow C \), where \( \eta (1) \equiv 1 \in C \). The *reduced comultiplication* induced by \( \eta  \) is 
\[
\bar{\Delta} = \Delta  - 1 \otimes id - id \otimes 1
\]


** Conilpotent coalgebra

A coalgebra \( C \) is *conilpotent* if for all \( x \in  C \), there is some \( n \) such that 
\[
\Delta^{n} (x) = 0
\]


** Cofree conilpotent coalgebra

Let \( C \) be a vector space. The cofree conilpotent coaugmented coalgebra \( T^{c}(C) \) has underlying vector space \( T(V) \) , the tensor product on \( V \), and coproduct
\[
\Delta (v_{1} \ldots v_{n}) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} v_{1} \ldots v_{i} \otimes v_{i+1} \ldots v_{n}
\]
coaugmentation \( \eta (1) = 1 \in V^{0} \cong k\), and counit
\[
\varepsilon (x) = 0
\]
for \( x \ne 1 \) and \( \varepsilon (1) = 1 \).

*** Notes

=Coaugmented:= This isn't just a coalgebra, it's coaugmented. So we can take \( \overline{T^{c} (V)} \cong \bigoplus_{n > 0} V^{\otimes n} \).

=Note:= The order zero and nth order terms will be \( 1 \otimes v_{1} \ldots v_{n} \) and \( v_{1} \ldots v_{n} \otimes  1 \). Removing these creates the reduced coalgebra, which is *conilpotent*! This is because the reduced comultiplication will always *reduce the number of terms* in each tensor factor. After enough time, it'll bring one of the tensor factors down to having only \( 1 \), which will cause that term to be removed.

=Conilpotent:= \( T^{c}(V) \) is *not* the cofree object in coalgebras! It's only the cofree object in *conilpotent* coalgebras. The actual cofree object has as underlying vector space 
\[
\prod_{n} V^{\otimes n} 
\]
which allows formal power series in elements of \( V \).

** Bar construction

Let \( A \) be an augmented dga. The bar construction on \( A \) is the coaugmented coalgebra
\[
B(A) = T^{c}(s \bar{A})
\]
where \( (s \bar{A})_{n} \equiv \bar{A}_{n-1} \)
viewed as a dgcoa, with differential \( d = d_{1} + d_{2} \), where 
\[
d_{1}(x_{1} \otimes  \ldots \otimes x_{n}) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} (-1)^{|x_{1}| + \ldots + |x_{i-1}|} x_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes d_{A}(x_{i} )\otimes \ldots \otimes x_{n}
\]

\[
d_{2}(x_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes x_{n}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} (-1)^{|x_{1}| + \ldots + |x_{i}|} x_{1} \otimes \ldots x_{i} x_{i+1} \otimes  \ldots \otimes x_{n}
\]

*** Notes

Because we've shifted the degrees, when we write \( |x_{i}| \), we really mean the shifted degree, which is \( \deg (x_{i} )+ 1 \), where \( \deg \) means the original degree

** Cobar construction

Let \( C \) be an coaugmented dgcoa. The cobar construction on \( C \) is the augmented algebra
\[
\Omega(C) = T(s^{-1}  \bar{C})
\]
where \( (s^{-1}  \bar{C})_{n} \equiv \bar{C}_{n+1} \)
viewed as a dgcoa, with differential \( d = d_{1} + d_{2} \), where 
\[
d_{1}(x_{1} \otimes  \ldots \otimes x_{n}) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} (-1)^{|x_{1}| + \ldots + |x_{i-1}|} x_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes d_{C}(x_{i} )\otimes \ldots \otimes x_{n}
\]

\[
d_{2}(x_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes x_{n}) =\sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum (-1)^{|x_{1}| + \ldots + |x_{i, (1)}|} x_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes x_{i, (1)} \otimes  x_{i, (2)} \otimes  \ldots \otimes x_{n}
\]

*** Notes

An easier way to write this is that \( d_{1} = d_{d_{C}} \) and \( d_{2} = d_{\Delta_{C}} \), using the notation that \( d_{f} \) sends the linear map \( f \) through all of the tensor factors, picking up appropriate signs

** Augmented DG Operad

An augmented DGO is an operad in chain complexes \( \mathcal{O} \) along with a map of operads
\[
\varepsilon : \mathcal{O} \rightarrow \mathcal{K}
\]
where \( \mathcal{K} \) is the operad with \( \mathcal{K}[1] = k \) and \( \mathcal{K}[n \ne 1] = 0 \)

*** Notes

This allows you to disregard the stuff in arity one from the operad. This is useful for the same reason that it is in the bar-cobar construction in dgas --- you don't want identity maps to allow your decompositions to be infinite when you pass to the cooperad.

\bar{\Delta} : \bar{C} \otimes \bar{C} \rightarrow \bar{C}

** (Co)fibrant resolutions

Let \( A \) be an augmented DGA then 
\[
\Omega B(A) \xrightarrow{\sim} A
\]
(ie they're quasi-isomorphic)

Similarly, if \( C \) is a conilpotent DGcoA, then 
\[
C \xrightarrow{\sim} B \Omega (C)
\]

*** Notes

=Resolutions:= Since we have these quasi-isomorphisms, we get a resolution of any (co)augmented DGcoA. The main issue is that when you perform this resolution, the space you get out the other side is very unwieldy --- it's not messy, but it's still hard to deal with because they're so much (co)free structure

=Purpose:= What's the point of doing a resolution when it just gives you a more complicated object? The idea is that the more complicated object you get is more complicated because you *made it free* --- it no longer has any relations (the data of the relations basically became the data of all of the ways of breaking stuff down or putting it together). So *mapping* into/out of it becomes easier

=Ext/Tor:= For an augmented dga \( A \), the homology of the dual of the bar construction is the same thing as the ext!
\[
Ext_{A} (k,k) \cong H^{\bullet}(B(A)^{*})
\]
and 
\[
Tor^{A}(k,k) \cong H_{\bullet} (B(A))
\]


=Koszul duality:= In the case of Koszul algebras, the homology of the bar construction (the Tor) becomes concentrated in a single degree, allowing you to just study a sub-coalgebra, called the *Koszul dual coalgebra*. And in fact, taking the dual of this coalgebra gives you the *Koszul dual algebra* \( A^{!} \), whose cohomology is then the Ext!
