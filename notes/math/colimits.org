* Definitions

** Cones

Given a functor \(F: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}\), a *cone* of \(F\) is \((C, \{ \phi_{D }\}_{D \in \mathcal{D}})\) such that 
\[
\phi_{D}: C \rightarrow F(D)
\]
and for all \(f: D_{1} \rightarrow D_{2}\), 
\[
\phi_{D_{1}} f = \phi_{D_{2}}
\]


** Limits (cones)

Let \(F: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}\) be a diagram. A limit of \(F\) is a universal cone
\[
\lim F \rightarrow F(D)
\]

** Limits (Representables)

Let \(F: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}\) be a diagram. A *limit* of *F* is an object \(\lim F\) such that 
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(A, \lim F) \cong  \operatorname{NT}(\Delta (A), F)
\]
is an isomorphism for all \(A \in \mathcal{C}\), where \(\Delta \) is the constant diagram functor, which sends \(A\) to the constant functor at \(A\).

In other words, we have a natural isomorphism between presheaves
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(-, \lim F) \cong  \operatorname{NT}(\Delta (-), F) : \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \text{Set}
\]

** Limits (Constructive)

The limit of a diagram \(F: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}\) is the equalizer 

\[
\lim F \cong  eq( \prod_{D} F(D) \rightrightarrows \prod_{f: D \rightarrow E} F(E))
\]

where the first map projects onto the codomain with \(\pi_{E}\) and the second map is \(F(f) \pi_{D} \)

* Examples

** (Co)equalizers

An equalizer is the limit of the following diagram \( f,g: A \rightrightarrows B\). So it's a universal \((E, \phi)\) with \(E \xrightarrow{\phi} A \rightrightarrows B\).

** (Co)products

Products are the limits of discrete diagrams. They have a universal map into all of the factors of the product:
\[
\pi_{i} : \prod_{i} A_{i} \rightarrow A_{i}
\]


** Initial/terminal objects

The terminal object is the limit of the empty diagram. Because it has no conditions, it has a unique map from every other object into it.

** Pullbacks and pushouts

A pullback is the limit of a diagram of the form \(f_{1}, f_{2}: A_{1}, A_{2} \rightarrow B\). So it's the upper left corner of a commuting square.


*** Arrow category

We can use our arrow category calculus to express pullbacks and pushouts:

A pullback of two maps \( f_{i} : A_{i} \rightarrow B \) is a pair of maps \( \tilde{f_{1}}, \tilde{f_{2}} \) that form the commutative square
\[
[f_{2}, \tilde{f_{2}}  ] : \tilde{f_{1}}_{} \rightarrow f_{1}
\]
so \( \tilde{f_{1}} f_{2} = \tilde{f_{2}} f_{1} \), that's universal

So any other \( [f_{2}, \phi_{2}] : \phi_{1} \rightarrow f_{1} \) has a universal map \( \Psi \) with \( \Psi \tilde{f_{i}} = \phi_{i} \), which is to say \( [1, \Psi] : \phi_{i} \rightarrow \tilde{f}_{i} \)
