* Summary

=Intuition:= Kan extensions are something like *generating from a basis.* If you have some collection of nice objects, you want to extend to a full category of objects built up out of those nice building blocks

=Left Kan extension:= Uses colimits to generate by gluing generators together 

=Right Kan extension:= Uses limits to restrict the nice objects to 

* Definitions

** Left Kan extension

Let \(F: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}\) and \(G: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}\) be functors. A *left Kan extension* is a pair \((L, \eta)\), where 
\[
\eta : G \Rightarrow FL
\]
such that for any \(\phi: G \Rightarrow FX\) there's a unique map \(\delta : L \Rightarrow X\) with 
\[
\eta \delta_{L} = \phi
\]

*** Intuition

\(FL\) is the smallest thing larger than \(G\), so we map into \(FL\), and any other thing larger than \(G\) has to hit \(FL\) first 

=Notation:= I'm going to write \(L_{F}[G]\) to denote the *left Kan extension of \(F\) along \(G\).* This is backwards to the normal way that it's notated, but I like that we get a kind of Einstein summation thing happening:
\[
\varepsilon : G \Rightarrow F L_{F}[G]
\]


** Right Kan extension

Let \(F: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}\) and \(G: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}\) be functors. A *right Kan extension* is a pair \((R, \varepsilon )\), where 
\[
\varepsilon : FR \Rightarrow G
\]
such that for any other \(\phi: FX \Rightarrow G \), there's a unique map \(\delta : X \Rightarrow R \) with 
\[
\delta_{F} \varepsilon = \phi
\]

*** Intuition

\(FR\) is the largest thing smaller than \(G\), which is why we map into \(G\), with \(\varepsilon : FR \Rightarrow G\), and every other thing mapping into \(G\) has to first map into \(FR\)

=Notation:= I'll denote by \(R_{F}[G]\) the *right Kan extension of \(F\) along \(G\)*, so that we have \(\eta: F R_{F}[G] \Rightarrow G\)

* Examples

** Limits

Let \(1\) be the terminal category with a single object and identity map. Then taking \(F : \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{D}\) and \(*: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow 1\), we have 
\[
L_{F}[*] \cong \operatorname{colim} F
\]
and 
\[
R_{F}[*] \cong \lim F
\]

** Groups

Let \(G\) be a group and \(H \leq G\) be a subgroup. There is an inclusion \(i_{H}: BH \rightarrow BG \). 

Given a representation \(F: BH \rightarrow \text{Vec}\), the LKE is the *induced representation* 
\[
L_{F}[i_{H}] \cong k[G] \otimes_{k[H]} -
\]
and the RKE is the *coinduced representation* 
\[
R_{F}[i_{H}] \cong \operatorname{Hom}_{k[H]}(k[G], -)
\]

** Generating a vector space by a basis

We view a basis as a *discrete category* \(\mathcal{B}\). We view an individual vector space by a functor from the terminal category \(V: 1 \rightarrow \text{Vec}\) 

Now take \(*: \mathcal{B} \rightarrow 1\) and let \(k: \mathcal{B} \rightarrow \text{Vec}\) send every object to the ground field \(k\). Then
\[
L_{*}[k] \cong  V
\]

And \(\varepsilon : k \Rightarrow * L_{*}[k] \cong V \) sends  \(\varepsilon_{b} : k(b) \rightarrow V \) by mapping \(1 \in k\) to the basis element \(b \in V\).
