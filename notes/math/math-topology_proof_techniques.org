* Tube arguments

=Idea:= Take a bunch of local neighbourhoods around slices of a compact set, and then use compactness to glue all these slices together

** example: tube lemma

Let \(X,Y\) be topological spaces with \(Y\) compact. If \(U \subseteq X \times Y\) is open, then for any \(\{x_{0} \} \times Y \in U \), there is an open neighbourhood \(u \in V \subseteq X\) such that 
\[
V \times Y \subseteq U
\]

*** Proof

Since \(U\) is open, we can find open neighbourhoods in the product topology containing \((x_{0}, y)\) for any \(y \in Y\). In particular, we can take these neighbourhoods to be basis elements:
\[
(x_{0}, y) \in V_{y} \times W_{y} \subseteq  U.
\]

=Compactness:= Since \(Y\) is compact and \(\cup_{y} W_{y} = Y\), we can find a finite subcover \(\cup_{i} W_{y_{i}} = Y\).

=Intersection:= We take \(V = \cap_{i} V_{y_{i}}\) which is an open set (since it's a finite intersection) which contains \(x_{0}\). 

Now \((x, y) \in V \times Y\) implies \(x \in V_{y_{i}}\) for all \(y_{i}\), and \(y \in Y = \cup_{i} W_{y_{i}}\). So there's some \(y_{j}\) for which 
\[
(x,y) \in V_{y_{j}} \times W_{y_{j}} \subseteq U.
\]

Thus \((x_{0}, y) \in V \times Y \subseteq U.\)

*** Intuition

The tube lemma says that we can widen any slice in an open set into a tube, as long as the length of the slice is compact.

** Example: Projections with compactness are closed

Let \(Y\) be compact. Then for any space \(X\), the projection map \(\pi_{1} : X \times Y \rightarrow X\) is closed.

*** Proof

Let \(C \subseteq X \times Y\) be closed. We'll show that \(\pi_{1}(C)^{c} \) is open. Take any \(x_{0} \in \pi_{1} (C)^{c}\). Then 
\[
\{ x_{0} \} \times Y \subseteq (X \times Y) \setminus C = C^{c}.
\]

Since \(C\) is closed, \(C^{c}\) is open.

=Tube lemma:= We apply the tube lemma to get an open neighbourhood \((x_{0} , y ) \in V \times Y \subseteq C^{c} \). Note that if \( v \in \pi_{1}(C) \) then \((v,y) \in C\) , so \((v,y) \ne V \times Y \subseteq  C^{c}\) and thus \(v \ne V\). So
\[
V \cap \pi_{1}(C) = \varnothing. 
\]

We have thus found an open neighbourhood of \(x_{0}\) entirely contained in \(\pi_{1}(C)^{c} \). Thus \(\pi_{1}(C)^{c} \) is open.

*** Intuition

The idea of the proof is very simple: we take any point \(x_{0}\) outside of \(\pi_{1}(C) \) and consider the slice over it. We can then enlarge the slice to a tube. In the projection, the widening corresponds to creating an open neighbourhood around \(x_{0}\) which doesn't intersect \(\pi_{1} (C) \).
