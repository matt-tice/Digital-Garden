** Derivatives on a Manifold

Let $f: M \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$. The derivative of $f$ at a point $p$ is the derivative of the composite
$f \circ \phi^{-1}: \mathbb{R}^{n} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$.

** Tangent Space

The tangent space

linear functionals $\phi: C^{\infty} (M) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ that satisfy the Leibniz rule
$\phi(fg) = f(p) \phi(g) + \phi(f)g(p)$

*** Functional Analysis

Linear functionals on functions are basically the same data as distributions.

You can view all derivatives (including the higher order ones) as distributions as follows: take the continuous dual of smooth compactly supported functions
$(C_{c}^{\infty }(M))'$ 
and just look at the ones that are supported at exactly the point $p$.

Then the ordinary tangent space is equivalent to the subset that vanish on constant functions and have first-order support
- this space is called the *first-order homogenous distributions concentrated at $p$.*
