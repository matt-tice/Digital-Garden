* Definitions

** Comma categories

Let \(F: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}\) and \(G: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{E}\) be functors. The *comma category* \(F \downarrow G\) is the category with 

=Objects:= \((A,B,f)\) where \(f: F(A) \rightarrow G(B) \)

=Morphisms:= \((\phi, \psi): (A_{1}, B_{1}, f_{1}) \rightarrow (A_{2}, B_{2}, f_{2})\) making the relevant square commute: \(f_{1} G(\psi) = F(\phi) f_{2}\)

*** Convention

If one of the functors is the identity functor or an inclusion functor, we just write the relevant categories, instead of the functor itself. For example, \(I_{\mathcal{C}} : \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{C} \) and \(i: \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}\) has the comma category \((\mathcal{C} \downarrow \mathcal{D})\)

** Arrow category

Consider the comma category \(\mathcal{C} \downarrow \mathcal{C}\). Then objects are just morphisms in \(\mathcal{C}\) and morphisms are the commuting squares. This is the arrow category.

** Slice category

Let \(X\) be about object of \(\mathcal{C}\). Then the comma category \(\mathcal{C} \downarrow X\) has objects maps into \(X\) and the morphisms are commuting triangles. This is the slice category.

Dually \(X \downarrow \mathcal{C}\) is the coslice category
