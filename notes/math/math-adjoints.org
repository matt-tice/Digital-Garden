* Definitions

** Adjoint functors

A pair of functors \(F: \mathcal{C} \rightleftarrows \mathcal{D} : G\) are called *adjoint* if there is an equivalence
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(F,-) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(-,G)
\]
between these functors \(\mathcal{C}^{op} \times \mathcal{D} \rightarrow \text{Set} \).

In this case, \(F\) is called the *left adjoint* and \(G\) is the *right adjoint*

** Floor and ceiling

View \(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{R}\) as posets, and take the inclusion \(i: \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\). This functor has both a left and right adjoint --- the ceiling function and floor function
\[
\text{ceil} \dashv i \dashv \text{floor}
\]

=Notation:= Actually this kinda motivates writing things in the opposite direction: \(\text{floor} \vdash i \vdash \text{ceil}\), going from the smallest to the biggest

** Discrete / Indiscrete space

Let \(U: \text{Top} \rightarrow \text{Set}\) be the forgetful functor on topological spaces. Then the functors that grant a set the discrete or indiscrete topology are left/right adjoints respectively.
\[
\text{Discr} \dashv U \dashv \text{Indiscr}
\]

** Initial / terminal

Let \(1\) be the terminal category (the discrete category with one object). Then adjoints to \(*: \mathcal{C} \rightarrow 1\) correspond to initial / terminal objects in \(\mathcal{C}\):
\[
\text{Init} \dashv * \dashv \text{Term}
\]


** Limits / colimits

Let \(\mathcal{J}\) be an indexing category. Then the adjoints to the diagonal functor \(\Delta : \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}^{\mathcal{J}}\) which sends each object \(X\) to the constant functor \(\Delta_{X}\), are the functors picking out limits and colimits of each functor \(\mathcal{J} \rightarrow \mathcal{C}\):
\[
\operatorname{colim} \dashv \Delta \dashv \lim
\]
