* Ends

Let $F: C^{op} \times C \to \mathcal{V}$
A *wedge* w \to F is a collection of maps $w_A: w \to F(A, A)$ such that 

An *end* is a universal wedge

** Intuition

Think of the maps $w_A: w \to F(A, A)$ as something like the maps that make up a natural transformation

Because your end is a limit, you're basically looking at a collection {w_A} such that they are invariant under altering the components of F(A, B) independently 


* Coends

F(A, A) \to F(A, B)
\downarrow                  \downarrow
F(B, A) \to w

We denote the coend by
\int^A F(A, A)

** Intuition

The maps $w_{A}: F(A, A) \to w$ define your gluing

You essentially glue together the maps that change the components of F(A, B) independently.

This is meant to be something like the *tensor product of modules*
- In M \otimes N, you have \alpha m \otimes n = m \otimes \alpha n

Similarly, here you want to think of C (its morphisms) as your ring, and the functors as your modules.

** Left Kan Extensions

If we have F: C \to D  and K: C \to E

C \to D
\downarrow
E

The *left Kan extension of F along K* is 

(L_K F)[Y] = \int^X Mor(K(X), Y) \cdot F(X)

where \cdot is the copwoer
