#+title: foliations
#+filetags: :math:geometry:

* References


* Summary

A foliation is a way to stratify a space: you create a bunch of /layers/ of the space made up of submanifolds. Each layer is called a *leaf* of the foliation

=Construction:= Typically the way that you build a foliation is by working locally; at each point you pick which directions you're allowed to move in. Then you try to glue all of that structure together using *Frobenius's theorem* 

* Definitions

** Foliations

A *foliation of degree $k$* of an $n$-manifold $M$ is a partition of $M$ into connected, $k$-dimensional submanifolds such that for all $p \in M$, there is a local coordinate chart $(x^{i})$ where the leaves are defined by holding the last $n-k$ coordinates constant.

** Tangent distribution

A \( k \) dimensional tangent distribution on an \( n \) manifold is a \( k \)-dimensional subbundle of the tangent bundle --- a smooth map that assigns a subspace of dimension \( k \)  \( D_{p} \subseteq T_{p} M \) to all \( p \in  M \)

=Notation:= Distributions with the same dimension for each \( D_{p} \) are called *regular.* But you can also define irregular distributions

** Involutive distribution

A tangent distribution \( D \rightarrow M \) is *involutive* if for any two vector fields \( \phi , \psi \in \Gamma_{p}(D) \) the Lie bracket \( [\phi ,\psi ] \in  \Gamma_{p}(D) \) for all \( p. \)

** Integrable manifold

Given a regular \( k \) distribution \( D \) on a manifold \( M \), an *integral manifold* is a \( k \)-dimensional submanifold \( N \subseteq M \) such that 
\[
T_{p} N = D_{p}
\]
for all \( p \in N \)

** Integral distribution

A distribution \( D \) is *integrable* if for all \( p \in M \) there is an integral manifold \( N^{p} \) such that \( p \in  N^{p} \).

* Theorems

** Foliations from integrability

Let \( D \) be an integrable distribution on \( M \). Then there exists a foliation corresponding to \( D \)

For each point \( p \in M \), the leaf \( L_{p} \) is the union of all *connected integral manifolds* that contain \( p \)

** Frobenius's theorem

A smooth tangent distribution on a manifold is integrable if and only if it's involutive


* Examples

** General Relativity

Foliations act as a way to talk about time propagation in general relativity, where space and time are wrapped up together.

=Spacelike foliation:= Slices spacetime into a stack of hypersurfaces indexed by a time parameter

=ADM:= (Arnowitt-Deser-Misner formalism). Rewrites Einstein's equations as an initial value problem on the foliation, and then evolving it with respect to time.

=Globally hyperbolic:= You get a smooth foliation into Cauchy surfaces if and only if you're globally hyperbolic (contain no closed timelike curves)

** Poisson manifolds

The phase space in classical mechanics breaks into a *symplectic foliation*, where each leaf is a subspace where conserved quantities are *constant*.

=Geometric quantization:= You quantize the individual leaves of the foliation rather than trying to quantize the whole phase space at once

** Statistical mechanics

=Equilibrium:= You foliate into leaves of constant entropy (adiabatic surfaces) or constant temperature (isotherms)

=Caratheodory:= The second law of thermodynamics is actually equivalent to the existence of a particular foliation on your state space --- near any equilibrium state there are adjacent states you *cannot reach* without exchanging heat (which corresponds to jumping between leaves)

** Condensed matter

In materials like soap films or LCD displays, molecules assemble into 2D layers that slide over one another. This forms a foliation

=Topological defects:= Imperfections actually correspond to places where the folitation breaks down.

** String theory

=Dbranes:= Open strings have boundaries that are lower dimensional subspaces called Dbranes. You can have parallel Dbranes that are leaves of a foliation of spacetime

** Gauge theory

In Yang-Mills you have *gauge orbits*, which foliate the space of all possible field configurations. Gauge fixing then becomes picking a representative point on each leaf.
