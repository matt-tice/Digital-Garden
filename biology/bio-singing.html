Bernoulli Effect

With a narrow glottis, air passing through will actually pull the vocal folds together.

Subglottal pressure pushes the folds back apart. This cycle produces an alternating wave of air, called the glottal flow.

  • the open glottis allows air through; they crash into air molecules above the glottis, creating a pressure wave
  • as the glottis begins to close, the air through the glottis decreases; to make up for the lost air, the air right above the glottis gets pulled forward, lowering the pressure above the glottis.
  • the low pressure causes the folds to shut even faster.
  • while the folds are shut, the pressure wave continues to travel forward, leaving a low pressure region behind.

This is what allows opera singers to produce incredibly loud sound — they don't increase the subglottal pressure
