Summary

Dosage

Infectious dose: The amount of a pathogen you actually need to ingest in order to get sick.

Low dose: Norovirus

High dose: Most common bacteria require thousands to millions of cells to subvert your immune system (this is why people don't constantly get sick)

Natural sterilization

UV radiation:

Human pathogens are very sensitive; they are used to living inside of a warm, wet, dark environment, not outside.

Desiccation: Pathogens need moisture to survive. The wind, open air, evaporation, kill off pathogens faster than what happens inside on plastic surfaces

Dilution

Every step an animal takes will wipe away a huge amount of the germs

It usually takes thousands to millions of cells to actually get you sick

Specialization

Organisms outside evolved to live outside, not in the human body

Moisture

Bacteria and fungi need water to replicate. As soon as a surface is dry, they stop replicating.

On the other hand, they don't need water to survive. They often enter a dormant state in the absence of water:

Shields

Spores: Bacteria like clostridium difficile can form hard protective shells called spores

Biofilms: Multiple bacteria cells can group together to form a biofilm which is highly resistant to drying out.

Envelopes: Influenza and coronavirus have outer, fatty membranes. These membranes are fragile, and only last hours or days. These are the viruses that alcohol-based hand sanitizer is effective on; it will dissolve the envelope.

Proteins: Norovirus don't have an envelope, but they have an outer shell of proteins that helps prevent them from drying out. Norovirus is remarkably potent; its outer shell helps it survive in your stomach acid, and eventually reach your intestines

Surfaces

Non-porous: Steel, plastic, glass. Allow droplets to pool together and dry very slowly. Phones, keyboards, doorknobs, pushbars, bus rails.

Porous: Fabric, paper, cardboard. Draw moisture away from pathogens immediately.

  1. Transfer efficiency

    Only a small percent of the germs on a surface will stick to your skin when you touch it

    Dry hands: Even fewer germs will stick to dry hands.

Life cycle

Germs will degrade over time. Even if they're still present on your smartphone, they can get inactivated

Viruses: Viruses are tiny capsules that contain genetic code. If the capsule is damage or the code is corrupted, it becomes harmless.

Capsid denaturation: The protective protein shell of a virus is called a capsid. The capsid contains receptor-binding proteins that will interact with your cells. Desiccation and heat will warp these proteins, causing them to no longer be able to bind to your cells

Genome fragmentation: UV light and oxidation will cause the DNA or RNA in a virus to be destroyed, rendering the virus incapable from using your cells to replicate

Membrane rupture: Soap and alcohol-based hand sanitizers will dissolve the envelope of enveloped viruses, causing them to fall apart.

Nutrients

Washing dishes

If you're washing dishes and food water splashed on you, it will disperse through the fabric of your clothes, increasing the surface area, and making it easier for it to evaporate. Your body heat, along with the airflow will help the splash dry.

Dishwater containing microscopic food particles actually doesn't contain enough nutrients to fuel a population explosion of pathogens. You need something like a towel soaked in milk

Bird poop

There are dangerous diseases linked to bird poop: histoplasmosis or cryptococcosis. But actually, they aren't carried by the bird — they're caused by fungi that live in the dirt

Chemicals: Bird poop is high in nitrogen and nutrients. When it lands in the soil, it can help the fungus in the soil grow

Fungus: The actual danger of the fungus comes from disturbing the spores. You have to shovel up the soil or something.

Body temp: Birds have much higher body temperatures than humans (around 40 to 42 degrees celsius, compared to our 37)

Dried: Dried bird poop that's been in the sun and wind has had most of its bacteria destroyed.

Hand washing

Proper 30sec hand washing achieves between 99.99% and 99.9999% reduction in pathogen population for norovirus. For many other pathogens, it's even more effective!

Transients: Hand washing works by removing transient pathogens (the ones that live on the very surface of the skin; which you picked up from things like doorknobs).

Residents: This flora is your body's natural protection. They live deep inside your pores, hair follicles, and skin ridges. Soap doesn't penetrate that far, allowing this flora to remain safe.

Stratum corneum: The outer layer of your skin, made up of dead skin cells, connected by lipids.

Acid mantle: A slightly acidic film produced by your skin, made of sebum and sweat. It helps prevent the growth of bacteria. It replenishes itself about 20 to 30min after hand washing.

Norovirus

Norovirus causes gastroenteritis. It causes diarrhea, vomiting, and stomach pain, and is one of the most highly infectious viruses in the world. But symptoms only last a couple days.

Immunity: Once you get norovirus, you're immune for around 6 months to 2 years. On top of that around 20% of Caucasians are naturally immune to norovirus!
