Summary

The brain consumes about 20% of our energy. When the brain has an energy deficit, it can trigger a headache

Pain

The brain itself cannot feel pain

  • pain receptors are called nociceptors

The pain comes from blood vessels, muscles, nerves, and membranes wrapping the brain

Vision

30%-50% of our brain is for processing vision

Trigeminal Nerve System

The main sensory system for your face / head. The nerves wrap tightly around the meningeal blood vessels, making the blood vessels very sensitive triggers for pain

  • usually nerve fibers run parallel to blood vessels

The TNS sends a signal to the brainstem, which goes to the thalamus

Inflammation

Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide

CGRP is an inflammatory neuropeptide

  • causes blood vessels in the meninges to dilate and swell
  • this in turn pushes on pain fibers

As the heart pumps blood through the swollen vessels, you end up getting a rhythmic pain sensation (throbbing)

Central Sensitization

People with chronic headaches get their neurons hyper-reactive, making even minor things like standard light become painful
