Language, Truth, and Logic (1936)

Goal: The goal of philosophy is not to establish first principles, nor is it to vindicate scientific statements. Philosophy is fundamentally linguistic — the propositions in philosophy are not factual. Philosophy is not scientific (ie not empirical). It is about uncovering hidden logical structure

Kinds: Philosophy is about types of expressions

Russell

Descriptions:

For Ayer, philosophy reveals a hidden logical structure: we start with ordinary language, and reveal the underlying logic contained within our statements.

Science

Science is made up of speculation (the realm of the scientists) and logic (the realm of the philosophers). But the speculative part is actually the essence of science: in order to be good at the philosophy of science, one must first "understand science" in the speculative sense:

"…if anything is to be achieved in the philosophy of physics, it must come from the inside…"

Metaphysics

Logical positivism thinks metaphysics is nonsense. Only verifiable (empirical) propositions are meaningful; this makes metaphysics nonsense ipso facto: it purports to be about non-empirical things.
