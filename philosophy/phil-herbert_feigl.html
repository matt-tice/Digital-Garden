Chance and law: an epistemological analysis of the roles of probability and induction in the natural sciences (1927)

Feigel's PhD thesis, inspired by Edgar Zilsel's Das Anwendungsproblem (1916)

Hume: Feigel used pragmatism

Induction: The problem of induction can't be solved using probability. We can only vindicate, rather than validate induction. That is, we cannot actually prove that induction is logical. We can only attempt to justify its use.

For Feigel probability depends on induction, not the other way around. Induction must be taken as a pragmatic axiom.

Frequency: Feigel used a frequency interpretation of probability.

Theorie und Erfahrung in der Physik (1929)

Feigel considers quantum mechanics and criticizes both Kantianism and conventionalism

Realist: Feigel argues for a critical realist approach to the relationship between theory and experience in physics. He actually did believe that scientific theories described real things in the world.

Logical Positivism: A New Movement in European Philosophy (1931)

Written with Blumberg

Positivism: LP is viewed as the synthesis of two traditions: the positivistic-empirical and mathematical logic. We must distinguish between analytic and synthetic propositions.

Logic: Like Wittgenstein, Feigel and Blumberg saw logic, by its very nature, as being tautological; it is only speaking about the internal structure of language. This is in contrast to empiricist approaches to logic and math.

Erkenntnis: "Knowledge proper". Objectivity.

Erlebnis: "Immediate experience". Subjectivity.

Knowledge: Is defined as the communicable: it communicates structure
